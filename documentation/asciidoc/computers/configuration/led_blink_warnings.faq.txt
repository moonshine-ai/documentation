Q: My Pi won't boot and the little LED is blinking. What does that mean?
A: The LED flashes long and short patterns to indicate specific failure statuses.

Q: How does the LED blink pattern actually work? Like what am I looking at?
A: It blinks long flashes then short flashes, repeating after a two-second gap.

Q: The light on my Pi keeps flashing over and over. Is that normal when something's wrong?
A: Yes, the pattern repeats after a two-second gap to help you identify the error.

# LED warning flash codes

Q: My Pi's LED is doing three short flashes and nothing else. What's wrong with it?
A: Three short flashes with no long flashes means a generic failure to boot.

Q: I'm getting four short blinks on startup. What does that mean?
A: Four short flashes means the start.elf file wasn't found on your card.

Q: The LED is flashing seven times short. Do I have a kernel problem?
A: Yes, seven short flashes means the kernel image wasn't found.

Q: I see eight short flashes. Is my RAM fried or something?
A: Eight short flashes indicates an SDRAM failure on your Raspberry Pi.

Q: What's the difference between eight short flashes and nine short flashes?
A: Eight means SDRAM failure; nine means there's insufficient SDRAM available.

Q: My Pi's LED does one long flash then two short ones. What's going on?
A: One long and two short flashes means SD card overcurrent was detected.

Q: I'm getting two long flashes then one short flash. What does that mean?
A: Two long, one short flash means the partition isn't formatted as FAT.

Q: Two long flashes and two short ones — is that a corrupted SD card?
A: That pattern means the Pi failed to read from the partition on your card.

Q: I've got a Pi 4 and it's doing two long flashes then four short ones. What's that?
A: That means there's a file signature or hash mismatch, specific to Pi 4 and 5.

Q: Three long and one short flash on my Pi 4 — is that an EEPROM issue?
A: Yes, three long and one short flash indicates an SPI EEPROM error on Pi 4 and 5.

Q: What does three long flashes and two short ones mean on a Pi 5?
A: It means the SPI EEPROM is write protected, specific to Pi 4 and Pi 5.

Q: My Pi's blinking four long and three short. I can't find anything about RP1. What's wrong?
A: Four long, three short flashes means the RP1 chip was not found.

Q: Four long flashes and four short ones — what's an unsupported board type mean exactly?
A: Not specified in this section.

Q: I'm seeing four long and six short flashes. Is my Pi not getting enough power?
A: Four long, six short flashes indicates a Power failure type A on your Pi.

Q: What's the difference between power failure type A and type B on the LED codes?
A: Type A is four long, six short flashes; type B is four long, seven short flashes.

Q: My Pi shows ten short flashes. Does that mean it's just turned off on purpose?
A: Ten short flashes means the Pi is in a HALT state.
