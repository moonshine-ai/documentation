# Support for HDMI monitors

Q: My Pi Zero has a tiny HDMI port and my cable won't fit. What's going on?
A: The Pi Zero has a mini HDMI port requiring a mini-to-full-size adapter.

Q: I've got a Raspberry Pi 4 and I want 4K at 60Hz. Is that even possible?
A: Yes, connect to HDMI0 and set hdmi_enable_4kp60=1 in config.txt.

Q: Does my Pi 4 support two 4K monitors at the same time?
A: Yes, but only at 30Hz refresh rate with two 4K displays simultaneously.

Q: I just got a Pi 5 and want two 4K screens. Do I need to change any settings?
A: No, Pi 5 supports two 4K displays at 60Hz with no extra configuration.

Q: My Pi 4B has two ports but they're not full size. What cables do I need?
A: You need micro-HDMI-to-full-size-HDMI leads for each display you attach.

Q: Does Raspberry Pi OS just automatically pick the best resolution for my monitor?
A: Yes, it uses the highest resolution and refresh rate your monitor supports.

Q: I want to connect two screens to my Pi. Which models actually support that?
A: Flagship models since Pi 4B, CM4 and later, and Keyboard models support two displays.

# Set resolution and rotation

Q: I want to change my screen resolution. Where do I find that setting on the desktop?
A: Open Control Centre from Preferences, then go to the Screens tab.

Q: I changed my resolution but I'm worried I'll mess something up. Can I undo it?
A: Yes, changes revert automatically if you don't confirm within ten seconds.

Q: I heard there was something called arandr for display settings. Should I use that?
A: No, the latest Raspberry Pi OS no longer supports arandr or raindrop.

Q: Will my display settings stick after I restart the Pi?
A: Yes, settings configured through Control Centre persist across a reboot.

Q: I've got two monitors connected. How do I pick which one to configure?
A: Right-click the screen outline in the panel or select it from the Screens list.

# Console resolution and rotation

Q: I'm running the Lite version with no desktop. How do I change the screen resolution?
A: Configure Kernel Mode Setting through the kernel command line settings.

Q: I've got two screens in console mode but the rotation is wrong on one. Can I fix just that one?
A: No, all connected displays share the same rotation settings in console mode.

Q: What's KMS? Do I need to know about it to change resolution without a desktop?
A: KMS stands for Kernel Mode Setting, used to configure resolution in console mode.

# Whole Document

Q: I've got a Pi 4 and a Pi 5. Which one handles dual 4K displays better?
A: Pi 5 does 4K at 60Hz on both; Pi 4 maxes out at 30Hz for dual 4K.

Q: I want to rotate my screen. Can I do that through the desktop or do I need the terminal?
A: Desktop users use Control Centre; terminal-only users configure the kernel command line.
