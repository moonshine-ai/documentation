# HDMI mode

Q: How do I change my HDMI settings on a Raspberry Pi?
A: Use the Screen Configuration utility or KMS video settings in cmdline.txt.

Q: My Pi 4 won't do 1366x768 at 60Hz. What's going on?
A: Pi 4 can't support DMT mode 81 and drops to the next advertised monitor mode.

Q: Why does my Pi 4 drop to 1280x720 instead of my monitor's native resolution?
A: Your monitor likely uses DMT mode 81, which Pi 4 can't support due to odd timings.

Q: Does the Pi 5 have the same problem with weird HDMI timings as the Pi 4?
A: No, Pi 5 has special handling for odd timings and supports those modes directly.

Q: I heard Pi 4 can do 4K at 60fps. Is that true?
A: Yes, Pi 4 supports up to 4Kp60 using dual displays and two output pixels per clock.

# Composite video mode

Q: Where's the composite video output on a Pi Zero?
A: The Pi Zero has an unpopulated TV header for composite video output.

Q: I've got an old CRT TV. Can I plug my Raspberry Pi into it?
A: Yes, most Pi models have composite video output, but Keyboard models do not.

Q: Where do I find the composite output on a Raspberry Pi 5?
A: It's on the J7 pad located next to the HDMI socket.

Q: Does the Pi 400 have composite video output?
A: Not specified in this section — Keyboard models have no composite output at all.

# `enable_tvout`

Q: I want to use composite video on my Pi 4 but I can't get it to work. What am I missing?
A: Set enable_tvout=1 in config.txt, but note this disables HDMI output too.

Q: Can I use both HDMI and composite video at the same time on a Pi 4?
A: No, enabling composite output on Pi 4 disables HDMI output.

Q: How do I actually turn on composite output on my Pi? Like what do I edit?
A: Add comma composite to the dtoverlay=vc4-kms-v3d line in /boot/firmware/config.txt.

Q: My Pi is outputting composite in NTSC but I need PAL. How do I switch that?
A: Add vc4.tv_norm=PAL to the single line in /boot/firmware/cmdline.txt.

Q: What video formats can I use for composite output? Is PAL one of them?
A: Yes, supported formats include NTSC, PAL, PAL-M, PAL-N, PAL60, SECAM, and others.

Q: What's the default composite video format on Raspberry Pi?
A: The default composite output format is NTSC video.

# LCD displays and touchscreens

Q: I don't want my Pi to use the touchscreen display even though it's connected. Can I disable it?
A: Yes, set ignore_lcd=1 to skip detection and prevent the LCD from being used.

Q: How do I turn off just the touch part of my Pi touchscreen but keep the display working?
A: Set disable_touchscreen=1 to disable only the touchscreen component of the display.

# `disable_fw_kms_setup`

Q: My HDMI display isn't working right and I think the Pi picked the wrong video mode. How do I fix it?
A: Set disable_fw_kms_setup=1 so the Linux KMS system parses the EDID and picks the mode instead.

Q: What does the firmware actually do with my HDMI display when the Pi boots up?
A: It reads the EDID, picks a video mode, then passes resolution and frame rate to the Linux kernel.

Q: Does Pi 5 pass video settings to the kernel on boot the same way older Pis do?
A: No, on Pi 5 disable_fw_kms_setup defaults to 1, so KMS handles mode selection itself.

# Whole Document

Q: I've got a Pi 4 and want to hook it up to an old TV. What are my options?
A: Enable composite output with enable_tvout=1, but this disables HDMI on Pi 4.

Q: What display options does a Raspberry Pi support in general?
A: Raspberry Pi supports HDMI, composite video, LCD touchscreens, and various video mode settings.

Q: I'm not sure if my Pi model supports composite video. How do I check?
A: All models have composite output except Keyboard models like the Pi 400 and Pi 500.
