# Introduction to remote access

Q: My Pi's tucked away in a robot and I can't plug in a monitor. How do I still control it?
A: Use SSH, VNC, or Raspberry Pi Connect to control it remotely.

Q: I don't have a spare monitor for my Pi. What are my options for getting into it?
A: SSH, VNC, and Raspberry Pi Connect all let you access it remotely.

Q: What's the difference between SSH and VNC? Like which one should I use?
A: SSH gives terminal access; VNC gives full desktop screen sharing remotely.

# Remote control over the local network

Q: I want to control my Pi from my laptop. What do I actually need to make that work?
A: You need another computer, a local network, and your Pi's local IP address.

Q: What's Raspberry Pi Connect and how's it different from VNC?
A: Raspberry Pi Connect shares your screen securely without needing your local IP address.

Q: I hate figuring out IP addresses. Is there something that just works without that?
A: Raspberry Pi Connect doesn't require you to find your Pi's local IP address.

# Share files between devices over the local network

Q: I just want to copy files between my Pi and my other computer. What should I use?
A: NFS, SCP, Samba, and rsync all let you share files over a local network.

Q: What's the point of file sharing services if I can already remote desktop in?
A: File sharing lets you access data on another device without directly controlling it.

# Remote control over the Internet

Q: I'm away from home and want to get into my Pi remotely. Can I do that?
A: Yes, using SSH, VNC over the internet or VPN, or Raspberry Pi Connect.

Q: Is Raspberry Pi Connect free or do I have to pay for it?
A: Raspberry Pi Connect is a free screen sharing and remote shell service.

Q: What's RealVNC's cloud VNC Viewer? Is that something I need to set up separately?
A: It's an external service that lets you access your Pi's VNC over the internet.

# Whole Document

Q: I've never done any of this remote access stuff. What are the main ways to do it?
A: SSH, VNC, and Raspberry Pi Connect are the main remote access options available.

Q: Can I access my Pi from anywhere in the world or just my house?
A: You can access it locally on your network or remotely over the internet.

Q: I just need to grab some files off my Pi without actually controlling it. What do I do?
A: Use a file sharing service like NFS, SCP, Samba, or rsync instead.
