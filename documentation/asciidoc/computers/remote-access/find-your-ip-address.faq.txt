# Desktop

Q: I've got a monitor plugged in — how do I quickly see my Pi's IP address?
A: Hover over the network icon in the system tray to see it.

Q: Where exactly do I look on the desktop to find my IP address?
A: A tooltip over the network icon shows your network name and IP address.

# Command line

Q: What's the quickest command to find my Pi's IP address from the terminal?
A: Run `hostname -I` and it outputs your local IP address.

Q: I just want to type something fast and get my IP. What do I type?
A: Type `hostname -I` in the terminal to get your local IP address.

# Boot output

Q: I boot to the command line instead of the desktop — will I still see the IP address somewhere?
A: Yes, your IP address appears in the last few boot messages before login.

# Network Manager

Q: What does `nmcli device show` actually tell me — is that how I find my IP?
A: Yes, it shows detailed info about all your network interfaces including IP addresses.

Q: I ran that nmcli command and got a wall of text. How do I find my actual IP in there?
A: Look for `IP4.ADDRESS[1]` in the wifi or ethernet block, depending on your connection.

Q: I'm connected over Wi-Fi — which part of the nmcli output do I even look at?
A: Find the block where `GENERAL.TYPE` says "wifi" and check `IP4.ADDRESS[1]`.

Q: What's that slash and number at the end of the IP address in nmcli output?
A: You can ignore the trailing slash and number, like `/24`, in those fields.

# Resolve `raspberrypi.local` with mDNS

Q: Do I actually have to know the IP address or can I just use a name to find my Pi?
A: You can ping `raspberrypi.local` and it'll show the IP address if reachable.

Q: I changed my Pi's hostname — does that break the dot-local thing?
A: No, Avahi automatically updates the `.local` mDNS address when you change the hostname.

Q: I don't remember what I named my Pi. How do I find it on the network?
A: Install Avahi on another device and use `avahi-browse` to browse all local hosts.

Q: What's the default hostname I'd use with that ping raspberrypi.local trick?
A: The default hostname on a fresh Raspberry Pi OS install is `raspberrypi`.

# Check your router's list of devices

Q: Can I just log into my router to find which device is my Pi?
A: Yes, log into your router's control panel and browse the connected devices list.

Q: I can't figure out which device in the router list is my Pi. Any tips?
A: If wired, filter for wired devices — there should be fewer to sort through.

Q: Where do I even find my router's IP address to log in?
A: It's often `192.168.1.1`, or check the label printed on your router.

# Find devices with `nmap`

Q: I'm on a Mac and want to use nmap — where do I get it?
A: Download it from the nmap.org download page.

Q: How do I actually scan my whole network with nmap to find my Pi?
A: Run `sudo nmap -sn 192.168.1.0/24`, replacing the subnet to match your network.

Q: The nmap scan is taking forever — is that normal?
A: It can take up to a minute depending on your local network speed.

Q: How do I know what subnet to scan with nmap?
A: First find your own computer's IP address, then scan that same subnet range.

Q: After running nmap, how do I spot which result is my Raspberry Pi?
A: Look for the hostname `raspberrypi` in the output — it'll show the IP next to it.

# Find devices with a smartphone app

Q: Is there an app on my phone I can use to find my Pi's IP address?
A: Yes, the free Fing app for Android and iOS scans your network for devices.

Q: I'm using Fing — how do I actually find the Pi in the list?
A: Scroll to the entry with manufacturer "Raspberry Pi" — the IP shows bottom left.

Q: Does Fing show the MAC address too or just the IP?
A: It shows the IP in the bottom left and the MAC address in the bottom right.

# Whole Document

Q: I don't have a screen connected to my Pi — how do I find its IP address?
A: Use `hostname -I` via SSH, ping `raspberrypi.local`, nmap, your router, or the Fing app.

Q: Why do I even need to know my Pi's IP address?
A: You need it to connect remotely using SSH or VNC from another machine.

Q: I've got no display, no keyboard, nothing — what's the easiest way to find my Pi's IP?
A: Try pinging `raspberrypi.local` from another device on the same network.
