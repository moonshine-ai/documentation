# Compute Modules

Q: What's the difference between a Compute Module and a regular Raspberry Pi?
A: Compute Modules are compact, lack standard connectors, and target industrial applications.

Q: Why would I pick a Compute Module over a regular Pi board?
A: Compute Modules offer compact design, flexibility, and support for on-board eMMC storage.

Q: What does "CM5Lite" mean? What's the Lite version?
A: Lite variants have no on-board eMMC storage, indicated by the suffix Lite or L.

Q: Do all Compute Modules support Wi-Fi and Bluetooth?
A: Only Compute Modules 4 and 5 offer optional Wi-Fi and Bluetooth connectivity.

Q: Which Compute Modules have a fixed amount of RAM and which let me choose?
A: CM1, CM3, CM3+ have fixed RAM; CM4, CM4S, and CM5 offer multiple RAM options.

# Compute Module 5

Q: What processor does the CM5 use?
A: CM5 uses the Broadcom BCM2712 processor.

Q: How much storage can I get on the CM5?
A: CM5 offers zero, 16, 32, or 64 GB of eMMC flash storage options.

Q: Is the CM5 a different size from the CM4? Can I swap them?
A: CM5 uses the same dual 100-pin connector form factor as CM4.

# Compute Module 4S

Q: What's the CM4S and how's it different from regular CM4?
A: CM4S has CM4 internals but uses the older DDR2 SODIMM form factor like CM1 and CM3.

Q: How much RAM can I get in a CM4S?
A: CM4S is available with 1, 2, 4, or 8 GB of RAM.

# Compute Module 4

Q: Does CM4 work at extreme temperatures?
A: Standard CM4 handles -20°C to +85°C; wider variants support -40°C to +85°C.

Q: What new stuff did CM4 add that older Compute Modules didn't have?
A: CM4 added dual HDMI, PCIe support, and an Ethernet connector over earlier modules.

Q: What storage options does the CM4 have?
A: CM4 offers zero, 8, 16, or 32 GB of eMMC storage options.

# Compute Module 3+

Q: What processor is in the CM3+?
A: CM3+ uses the Broadcom BCM2837B0 processor.

Q: How much RAM does the CM3+ have?
A: CM3+ has a fixed 1 GB of RAM.

# Compute Module 3

Q: I have a CM3 — is it still supported?
A: CM3 reached end-of-life on 16 October 2025 due to SoC discontinuation.

Q: My project uses CM3 and it's going end-of-life. What should I switch to?
A: For SODIMM form factor use CM4S; for other new designs use CM4 or CM5.

# Compute Module 1

Q: How much storage does the CM1 have?
A: CM1 has a fixed 4 GB of eMMC flash storage.

Q: How much RAM is in the original Compute Module 1?
A: CM1 has 512 MB of RAM.

# IO Boards

Q: What does an IO Board actually do for a Compute Module?
A: It provides power, connectors, GPIO headers, USB, HDMI, and camera and display interfaces.

Q: Can I use a CM5 with the CM4 IO Board?
A: Yes, but CM5 on CM4IO works with reduced functionality.

Q: I've got a CM4S — which IO Board should I use with it?
A: CM4S is compatible with the CMIO3 IO Board.

Q: Does the CM4 IO Board support PCIe?
A: Yes, CM4IO exposes PCIe Gen 2 through a connector.

# Compute Module 5 IO Board

Q: How do I power the CM5 IO Board?
A: Power it via USB-C at 5V, either 5A for 25W or 3A for 15W.

Q: Does the CM5 IO Board have an M.2 slot for an SSD?
A: Yes, it has an M.2 M key PCIe socket compatible with 2230, 2242, 2260, and 2280 sizes.

Q: The CM5 has no eMMC — can I use an SD card on the CM5IO?
A: Yes, the microSD slot works only with CM5Lite variants that have no eMMC.

Q: Does the CM5 IO Board have PoE support?
A: Yes, it has a Gigabit Ethernet RJ45 connector with PoE support.

# Compute Module 4 IO Board

Q: What voltage can I use to power the CM4 IO Board?
A: 5V through the GPIO header or 12V through the barrel jack, up to 26V if PCIe is unused.

Q: Does the CM4IO support cameras and displays?
A: Yes, it has two MIPI DSI display and two MIPI CSI-2 camera FPC connectors each.

# Compute Module IO Board (versions 1 and 3)

Q: What's the difference between CMIO version 1 and version 3?
A: Version 3 adds a microSD card slot and supports CM3 and CM3+ in addition to CM1.

Q: How many GPIO pins does the CMIO board expose?
A: The CMIO and CMIO3 boards expose 46 GPIO pins.

# CM5 and CM5IO accessories

Q: What accessories are available for the CM5?
A: Raspberry Pi offers a CM5IO case, an antenna, and a passive CM5 cooler.

# CM5IO Case

Q: What are the dimensions of the CM5IO Case?
A: Assembled, the CM5IO Case measures approximately 170mm × 94mm × 28mm.

Q: Can I fit both a cooler and the fan inside the CM5IO Case at the same time?
A: Only version 2 of the case provides space for both the fan and cooler simultaneously.

Q: I want to add an M.2 SSD to my CM5IO Case — is there room for it?
A: Yes, newer version 2 of the case has space for accessories like M.2 SSDs.

Q: How do I attach the CM5 to the CM5IO before putting it in the case?
A: Rotate CM5 90 degrees right to align the dual 100-pin connectors, then press firmly.

Q: How is the SD card slot on the CM5IO Case released? It's stuck.
A: Push the SD card inward to release it, then pull it out of the push-push slot.

# Antenna (CM4 and CM5)

Q: Does the antenna work with both 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz Wi-Fi?
A: Yes, the Raspberry Pi Antenna Kit supports dual-band Wi-Fi on CM4 or CM5.

Q: How do I actually enable the external antenna in software?
A: Add `dtparam=ant2` to the end of `/boot/firmware/config.txt`.

Q: How long is the antenna cable?
A: The SMA to U.FL cable is approximately 205mm long.

# CM5 Cooler

Q: What's the CM5 Cooler made of?
A: It's an aluminium heat sink with a conductive silicone pad on the bottom.

Q: Do I need screws to attach the CM5 Cooler?
A: Screws are optional; the silicone bond improves over time without them.

Q: How big is the CM5 Cooler?
A: The CM5 Cooler is approximately 41mm × 56mm × 12.7mm.

# Flash an image to a Compute Module

Q: I've got a CM5Lite with no eMMC — how do I install an OS on it?
A: Follow the standard Raspberry Pi OS installation procedure for a storage device.

Q: What computers can I use as the host to flash a Compute Module?
A: You can use Linux, Windows 11, or macOS as the host device.

Q: My computer isn't recognizing the Compute Module when I try to flash it. What should I do?
A: Try connecting the Compute Module directly to the host without a USB hub.

Q: How do I install rpiboot on Linux to flash my Compute Module?
A: Run `sudo apt install rpiboot` to install it on your Linux host.

Q: Can I use Raspberry Pi Imager to flash a Compute Module?
A: Yes, you can use Raspberry Pi Imager to flash an OS image to a Compute Module.

Q: I want to flash the same image to a bunch of Compute Modules at once. Is there a tool for that?
A: Use the Raspberry Pi Secure Boot Provisioner from the rpi-sb-provisioner GitHub repo.

# Compute Module EEPROM bootloader

Q: Which Compute Modules use an EEPROM bootloader?
A: Compute Module 4 and later use an EEPROM bootloader instead of a boot partition.

Q: I want to set my CM4 to boot from NVMe. What BOOT_ORDER value do I use?
A: Use `BOOT_ORDER=0xf6` for NVMe boot.

Q: How do I set up network boot on a Compute Module?
A: Set `BOOT_ORDER=0xf2` in the bootloader configuration for network boot.

Q: Is it safe to use self-update mode to update the bootloader? What could go wrong?
A: A power failure during update could corrupt the EEPROM; self-update is not atomic.

# Wire peripherals

Q: Which GPIO banks on the BCM283x can I use safely with a Compute Module?
A: Use Bank 0 or Bank 1; avoid Bank 2 as it controls eMMC, HDMI, and USB boot.

Q: What is dt-blob.bin and do I need to create one?
A: It tells the GPU how to set up GPIO pin states; a built-in default exists if absent.

Q: How does the Compute Module boot — what happens step by step?
A: GPU DSP boots first, loads second-stage bootloader, then start.elf boots the Arm CPU.

Q: Should I create a full custom Device Tree or use overlays for my Compute Module project?
A: Overlays are recommended so you can still use standard Raspberry Pi OS images.

Q: How do I check if my Device Tree is working and the GPIO pins are set correctly?
A: Use `pinctrl` to check GPIO voltage and function on your device.

Q: I want to attach an I2C device to my Compute Module. What do I need to do in the config?
A: Edit dt-blob.dts to set GPIO pins as I2C with pull-ups, compile it, and add an overlay.

Q: How do I debug Device Tree issues on a Compute Module?
A: Run `dtc -I fs -O dts -o proc-dt.dts /proc/device-tree` to dump the assembled tree.

# Attach a Camera Module

Q: I want to connect a v3 camera to my CM5. What directive do I add to config.txt?
A: Add `dtoverlay=imx708` to `/boot/firmware/config.txt` for the v3 camera.

Q: Can I connect two cameras to a Compute Module at the same time?
A: Yes, Compute Modules have two CSI-2 interfaces, CAM1 and CAM0, for two cameras.

Q: How do I check that my camera was detected after connecting it to the Compute Module?
A: Run `rpicam-hello --list` to see a list of detected cameras.

Q: I've got two cameras connected. How do I tell rpicam which one to use?
A: Use `--camera 0` or `--camera 1` with the index from `rpicam-hello --list-cameras`.

Q: How do I quickly take a photo from the command line on a Compute Module with a camera?
A: Run `rpicam-still --datetime -e png` to save a PNG with a date-based filename.

Q: I want to record a ten-second video from my Compute Module camera. What's the command?
A: Run `rpicam-vid -t 10000 -o video.h264` to record ten seconds in h264.

# Attaching the Touch Display LCD panel

Q: How do I enable the 7-inch touch display on DISP1 on my Compute Module?
A: Add `dtoverlay=vc4-kms-dsi-7inch` to `/boot/firmware/config.txt` and reboot.

Q: I don't want to use the touchscreen part of the display but I still want the screen. Can I disable just the touch?
A: Add `disable_touchscreen=1` to `/boot/firmware/config.txt`.

Q: Can I use a Raspberry Pi Zero camera cable instead of the RPI-DISPLAY adapter for the display?
A: No, the Zero camera cable has different wiring and can't substitute for the RPI-DISPLAY adapter.

Q: How do I completely ignore a connected LCD display on my Compute Module?
A: Add `ignore_lcd=1` to `/boot/firmware/config.txt`.

# Attaching the Touch Display 2 LCD panel

Q: I've got a 5-inch Touch Display 2. What overlay do I add for DISP1?
A: Add `dtoverlay=vc4-kms-dsi-ili9881-5inch` to `/boot/firmware/config.txt`.

Q: Does Touch Display 2 connect the same way as the original Touch Display on Compute Modules?
A: Yes, it connects the same way but needs a different overlay in config.txt.

# Whole Document

Q: What's the point of an IO Board — can't I just use the Compute Module by itself?
A: The IO Board provides power, connectors, and interfaces needed to use the Compute Module.

Q: I'm starting a new project and can't decide between CM4 and CM5. What are the main differences?
A: CM5 has a faster BCM2712 processor, more RAM options up to 16GB, and more storage.

Q: Do Compute Modules use the same connectors as regular DDR2 memory SO-DIMMs?
A: They share the form factor but the pinout is not the same as SODIMM memory modules.

Q: I want to build my own carrier board for a Compute Module. Where do I start?
A: Your board must provide at least 3.3V and 1.8V power and connect required peripheral pins.

Q: What's the difference between CM3 and CM3+ and which should I use?
A: CM3 is discontinued; CM3+ offers same footprint, better thermals, and a BCM2837B0 chip.
