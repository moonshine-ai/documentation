# Introduction

Q: What's the difference between a Model A and a Model B Raspberry Pi?
A: Model B has Ethernet; Model A is smaller, cheaper, with less RAM and fewer USB ports.

Q: I'm trying to pick between a Pi Zero and a regular Pi. What's the main difference?
A: Zero series is smaller, cheaper, lower power, with fewer ports than flagship models.

Q: Does the Pico run Linux like other Raspberry Pis?
A: No, Pico is a microcontroller board that does not run Linux.

Q: What's a Compute Module and when would I use one instead of a regular Pi?
A: Compute Modules are for embedded or industrial use, without onboard connectors.

Q: My Pi is in a keyboard — what series is that?
A: Keyboard computers combine SBC hardware and ports inside a compact keyboard case.

Q: Does the Pico use an SD card for storage?
A: No, programs are flashed directly onto Pico's onboard memory instead.

# Flagship single-board computer (SBC) series

Q: How many GPIO pins does the Raspberry Pi 5 have?
A: Raspberry Pi 5 has a 40-pin GPIO header.

Q: I want to connect two monitors to my Pi 4. Is that possible?
A: Yes, Raspberry Pi 4 Model B has two micro-HDMI ports for dual display output.

Q: Does the Pi 3B+ support Wi-Fi and Bluetooth?
A: Yes, it has dual-band 802.11ac Wi-Fi and Bluetooth 4.2 with BLE support.

Q: What's the max RAM I can get on a Raspberry Pi 5?
A: Raspberry Pi 5 is available with up to 16 GB of RAM.

Q: I heard the Raspberry Pi 2 Model B is being discontinued. Is that true?
A: Revisions 1.1 and 1.2 reached End-of-Life October 2025; revision 1.3 is still recommended.

Q: What kind of power connector does the Pi 4 use?
A: Raspberry Pi 4 Model B uses a USB-C connector, requiring 5V at 3A.

Q: Does the Pi 5 have a PCIe slot for adding stuff like an NVMe drive?
A: Yes, Raspberry Pi 5 has a single-lane PCIe FFC connector onboard.

Q: What's the Ethernet speed on the Raspberry Pi 5?
A: Raspberry Pi 5 has Gigabit Ethernet at 1 Gb/s with PoE+ support.

# Keyboard computer (X00) series

Q: How much RAM does the Raspberry Pi 400 have?
A: The Raspberry Pi 400 has 4 GB of RAM.

Q: Does the Pi 500 support the same Wi-Fi speeds as the Pi 5?
A: Yes, both support dual-band 802.11ac Wi-Fi 5 at up to 300 Mb/s.

Q: What's the naming convention for the keyboard Pi models?
A: The number (e.g. 400, 500) corresponds to the equivalent flagship SBC model number.

Q: Does the Raspberry Pi 500+ have any built-in storage beyond the SD card?
A: Yes, the Pi 500+ includes an internal M.2 SSD with 256 GB of storage.

# Zero series

Q: I got a Pi Zero without any header pins. Do I need to solder them on myself?
A: Yes, models without "H" or "with headers" require you to solder pins manually.

Q: Does the Pi Zero 2 W have Bluetooth?
A: Yes, it has Bluetooth 4.2 with Bluetooth Low Energy support.

Q: What ports do all Pi Zero boards share in common?
A: All Zeros have a microSD slot, mini HDMI port, and two micro-USB ports.

Q: Does the original Pi Zero have a camera port?
A: Only from version 1.3 onwards; earlier versions lacked the CSI camera port.

# Compute Module series

Q: I'm building an industrial product and need a Pi without connectors sticking out. What should I use?
A: The Compute Module series is designed for that, connecting via a separate baseboard.

Q: What happened to the Compute Module 3?
A: CM3 and CM3 Lite reached End-of-Life on 16 October 2025 due to SoC discontinuation.

Q: What's the difference between Compute Module 4 and CM4S?
A: CM4S uses DDR2 SODIMM form factor while CM4 uses dual 100-pin high-density connectors.

Q: Does the Compute Module 5 support Wi-Fi?
A: Yes, optionally, with dual-band 802.11ac Wi-Fi 5 and Bluetooth 5 available.

Q: I see the CM3 uses a SODIMM form factor — can I plug it into a regular RAM slot?
A: No, despite the physical DDR2 SODIMM form factor, it's electrically incompatible.

# Pico microcontroller boards

Q: What's the difference between a Pico W and a regular Pico?
A: Pico W adds 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and Bluetooth 5.2, the regular Pico has neither.

Q: Does the Pico 2 have more memory than the original Pico?
A: Yes, Pico 2 has 520 kB RAM and 4 MB storage versus 264 kB and 2 MB on original Pico.

Q: I bought a Pico H — what does the H mean?
A: The H suffix means header pins come pre-soldered on the GPIO header.

Q: Does the Pico 2 W support Bluetooth?
A: Yes, it supports Bluetooth 5.2 with Bluetooth Low Energy.

# Schematics and mechanical drawings

Q: Where can I find mechanical drawings for the Raspberry Pi 5?
A: PDF mechanical drawings and a STEP file are available on the Raspberry Pi pip website.

Q: I need to design a case for a Pi 3A plus — are there case drawings available?
A: Yes, case drawings in PDF are available, and they also apply to the Pi 1 Model A+.

Q: Are schematics available for the Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W?
A: Yes, schematics, mechanical drawings, and test pad position documents are available.

Q: What does the RUN test pad on the Pi Zero 2 W do?
A: Connecting the RUN pad to GND resets the board.

Q: Where's the STATUS LED test pad located on the Pi Zero 2 W?
A: It's at coordinates 5.15 mm, 8.8 mm from the origin, and LOW means LED is ON.

# Product compliance and safety

Q: What flammability rating do Raspberry Pi boards have?
A: The PCBs adhere to UL94-V0 flammability rating; this applies to PCBs only.

Q: I'm building a product with a Pi inside and need to use the Raspberry Pi logo. How do I do that?
A: Apply online through the Powered by Raspberry Pi program to get permission to use the logo.

Q: Does Raspberry Pi offer any help navigating compliance testing for products I build?
A: Yes, the Compliance Support Programme connects you with test engineers and a UL team.

# Frequency management and thermal control

Q: My Pi is getting hot under load. Will it damage itself?
A: No, it throttles CPU and GPU frequencies automatically to stay under 85°C.

Q: At what temperature does the Pi start throttling?
A: Arm cores throttle progressively between 80°C and 85°C; GPU throttles at 85°C.

Q: I've heard the Pi 3B+ handles heat better. What changed?
A: It uses improved PCB technology for better heat dissipation and adds a soft 60°C limit.

Q: How do I actually check the temperature of my Pi right now?
A: Run `vcgencmd measure_temp` for an accurate, instantaneous SoC temperature reading.

Q: Do I need a heatsink to stop my Pi from overheating?
A: No, built-in throttling prevents damage, but heatsinks reduce throttling and improve performance.

Q: I want to reduce how much power my Pi 4 uses when it's idling. How do I do that?
A: Install cpufrequtils and set the CPU governor to powersave using `sudo cpufreq-set -g powersave`.

# Use DVFS

Q: What is DVFS and do I need to worry about it on my Pi 4?
A: DVFS dynamically lowers voltage with frequency, reducing heat; it works automatically on Pi 4.

Q: I've got a Pi 4 running headless. Is there a DVFS setting that saves power?
A: Set `dvfs=1` in config.txt for slight power savings, though it risks PCIe stability issues.

Q: Does the Pi 5 support the same DVFS settings as Pi 4?
A: No, DVFS settings were removed on 5-series devices; they always effectively use mode 3.

# Fan cases

Q: What fan options are available for the Raspberry Pi 5?
A: The Active Cooler and the official Raspberry Pi 5 Case both include compatible fans.

Q: At what temperature does the Pi 5 fan turn on?
A: The fan starts spinning at 50°C at 30% speed; it's off below 50°C.

Q: Where does the fan plug in on the Pi 5?
A: It plugs into the four-pin JST-SH PWM connector between the GPIO header and USB 2 ports.

Q: I'm planning to overclock my Pi 5. Which cooling solution should I use?
A: The Active Cooler is recommended for overclockers as it provides better cooling performance.

Q: Can I adjust the temperature thresholds for the Pi 5 fan?
A: Yes, use `fan_tempN`, `fan_tempN_hyst`, and `fan_tempN_speed` dtoverlay settings in config.txt.

Q: My Pi 5 fan runs at full speed. At what temperature does that happen?
A: The fan reaches 100% speed at 75°C and drops back when temperature falls 5°C below that.

# Raspberry Pi boot EEPROM

Q: Which Pi models use an EEPROM to boot instead of a bootcode.bin file?
A: Flagship models since Pi 4B, Compute Modules since CM4, and keyboard models since Pi 400.

Q: How do I update the bootloader on my Raspberry Pi 4?
A: Use raspi-config or Raspberry Pi Imager; Raspberry Pi OS also auto-updates for bug fixes.

Q: I accidentally messed up my bootloader config and now my Pi won't boot. What do I do?
A: Re-flash the bootloader EEPROM with a valid configuration to recover boot.

Q: How do I stop my Pi from automatically updating the bootloader?
A: Run `sudo systemctl mask rpi-eeprom-update` to disable the automatic update service.

Q: What's the difference between the "default" and "latest" bootloader releases?
A: Default gets critical fixes periodically; latest updates more often with newest features.

Q: How do I check what version of bootloader I'm currently running?
A: Run `vcgencmd bootloader_version` to see the current bootloader version.

Q: I want to edit my bootloader config. How do I open it?
A: Run `sudo -E rpi-eeprom-config --edit` to open the configuration in a text editor.

# Boot diagnostics

Q: My Pi 4 has no SD card inserted and I see a screen with info on my TV. What is that?
A: That's the boot diagnostics display, shown when the bootloader can't find bootable media.

Q: What does the QR code on the boot diagnostics screen link to?
A: It links to the Raspberry Pi software downloads page.

Q: How do I turn off the boot diagnostics screen that appears on HDMI?
A: Set `DISABLE_HDMI=1` in the bootloader configuration to disable it.

Q: My Pi shows boot diagnostics but I want to reboot it — do I have to unplug it?
A: You can power cycle the device or press and hold the power button if it has one.

# Raspberry Pi boot modes

Q: My old Pi 3 won't boot from a USB drive. What do I need to do first?
A: You need to enable USB host boot mode by setting `program_usb_boot_mode=1` in config.txt.

Q: I want to enable GPIO boot mode on my Pi 3B. Is that reversible?
A: No, setting OTP bits for GPIO boot mode is permanent and cannot be undone.

Q: What does enabling USB host boot mode permanently do to a Pi 3A+?
A: It permanently prevents that Pi from booting in USB device mode.

# USB mass storage boot

Q: Can I boot my Raspberry Pi 4 from a USB flash drive out of the box?
A: Yes, as long as USB boot is specified in the BOOT_ORDER configuration.

Q: My USB hard drive is really slow to start up and my Pi times out. Is there a fix?
A: Add a file called "timeout" to your SD card to extend the wait time to six seconds.

Q: I want to boot from USB on a Pi 3B. Does it work the same as on Pi 4?
A: No, you first need to enable USB host boot mode by programming the OTP memory.

Q: My Pi seems to ignore my USB drive and boots from SD card. Why?
A: If an SD card is present, the Pi boots from that first; remove it to boot from USB.

# Network booting

Q: Can I boot my Raspberry Pi over Wi-Fi?
A: No, network booting only works over the wired Ethernet adapter, not wireless.

Q: My Pi is trying to network boot but nothing's happening. How do I debug it?
A: Use tcpdump on your TFTP server to capture packets and check for DHCP requests.

Q: How many times does a Pi try DHCP before giving up on network boot?
A: It attempts DHCP five times with five-second intervals, totaling 25 seconds.

# NVMe SSD boot

Q: How do I make my Pi 5 boot from an NVMe SSD?
A: Use raspi-config under Advanced Options > Boot Order to include NVMe in the boot order.

Q: How do I check if my NVMe drive is being detected by my Pi?
A: Boot from an SD card and run `ls -l /dev/nvme*` to check for the device.

Q: I see `/dev/nvme0n1` but can't figure out the partitions. How do I check?
A: Run `lsblk` to see partition assignments for your NVMe drive.

# HTTP boot

Q: What's the minimum bootloader version I need for HTTP boot?
A: You need a bootloader released on or after 10th March 2022.

Q: Does HTTP boot work over Wi-Fi?
A: No, HTTP boot requires a wired Ethernet connection.

Q: I want to host my own network install image. Do I need to sign it?
A: Yes, you must sign the image and add your public key to the EEPROM.

# GPIO boot mode

Q: Which Pi models support GPIO boot mode?
A: Only Raspberry Pi 3A+, 3B, 3B+, Compute Module 3, and 3+ support GPIO boot mode.

Q: Once I enable GPIO boot mode, can I disable it later?
A: No, once OTP bits are set for GPIO boot mode, they cannot be unset.

Q: What GPIO bank should I use for GPIO boot mode on a regular Pi 3B?
A: Use Bank 1 (GPIOs 22-26); Bank 2 GPIOs are only available on Compute Modules.

# GPIO and the 40-pin header

Q: How do I find out what all the pins on my Pi's GPIO header do?
A: Open a terminal and run the `pinout` command to see a GPIO reference.

Q: Can I use any GPIO pin as a PWM output?
A: Software PWM works on all pins; hardware PWM is only on GPIO12, 13, 18, and 19.

Q: What voltage do the GPIO pins run at on a Raspberry Pi?
A: GPIO pins operate at 3.3V; outputs are set to 3.3V and inputs are 3.3V-tolerant.

Q: How much total current can I safely draw from all the GPIO pins together?
A: All GPIO pins combined can safely draw up to 50mA total.

Q: I accidentally connected a 5V device to a GPIO pin. Did I break my Pi?
A: The text doesn't address damage diagnosis; GPIO pins are only 3.3V-tolerant.

Q: Which GPIO pins have fixed pull-up resistors?
A: GPIO2 and GPIO3 have fixed pull-up resistors; others are configurable in software.

# GPIO pads control

Q: What's the default drive strength for GPIO pins?
A: The default drive strength is 8mA on BCM2835/2836/2837 devices.

Q: Is it safe to set GPIO drive strength to 16mA on all pins at once?
A: No, drawing 272mA total would collapse the 3.3V supply; use caution.

Q: What does setting a GPIO drive strength to 2mA actually mean?
A: It's the maximum current under which the pad meets voltage specifications, not a hard limit.

# Industrial use of the Raspberry Pi

Q: What command do I run to see all the OTP values on my Pi?
A: Run `vcgencmd otp_dump` to display all OTP register contents.

Q: Is it possible to undo changes I make to OTP memory?
A: No, OTP bits can only be changed from 0 to 1 and cannot be reversed.

Q: Where are the customer OTP rows located in the OTP dump on a non-BCM2712 Pi?
A: Customer OTP values are in rows 36 to 43, giving eight 32-bit rows.

Q: Can I lock the OTP customer rows so nobody can change them later?
A: Yes, but locking is irreversible on non-BCM2712 devices; BCM2712 locks reset on reboot.

# Raspberry Pi connector for PCIe

Q: How do I enable the PCIe connector on my Pi 5 for a non-HAT+ device?
A: Add `dtparam=pciex1` to `/boot/firmware/config.txt` and reboot.

Q: Can I enable PCIe Gen 3.0 speeds on my Pi 5?
A: Yes, but the Pi 5 isn't certified for Gen 3.0 and connections may be unstable.

Q: How do I make my Pi 5 boot from a PCIe device?
A: Edit the bootloader config, change BOOT_ORDER to include `6`, and add `PCIE_PROBE=1`.

# Power button

Q: How do I shut down my Pi 5 using the power button?
A: Briefly press the power button; on desktop a menu appears, or press twice for quick shutdown.

Q: I want to add an external power button to my Pi 5. Is that possible?
A: Yes, connect a Normally Open momentary switch across the two J2 jumper pads.

Q: I held the power button and nothing happened. What's a hard shutdown?
A: Press and hold the power button to force a hard shutdown.

# Power supply

Q: What power supply should I use for a Raspberry Pi 5?
A: Raspberry Pi recommends the official 27W USB-C Power Supply for Pi 5.

Q: My Pi keeps showing power warnings. What does that mean?
A: Supply voltage has dropped below 4.63V; switch to a higher quality supply and cable.

Q: How much USB power can my Pi 5 give to connected devices?
A: Up to 1.6A with a 5A supply, or only 600mA if using a 3A power supply.

Q: Does the Pi 5 support Power over Ethernet?
A: Yes, the Pi 5 Ethernet jack supports PoE+ (IEEE 802.3at-2009) with a compatible HAT.

Q: I want to reduce power consumption on my Pi 5 when it's switched off. How?
A: Set `POWER_OFF_ON_HALT=1` in EEPROM config to drop consumption to around 0.01W.

Q: My Pi 4 is being powered through a USB hub connected to it. Is that a problem?
A: Yes, back-powering bypasses protection circuitry and can cause damage during a power surge.

# Real Time Clock (RTC)

Q: Does the Raspberry Pi 5 have a built-in real-time clock?
A: Yes, the Raspberry Pi 5 includes an RTC module with a battery connector.

Q: Can I set my Pi 5 to wake up automatically at a scheduled time?
A: Yes, set a wake alarm via `/sys/class/rtc/rtc0/wakealarm` and then halt the board.

Q: What battery should I use for the Pi 5 RTC?
A: Use a rechargeable lithium manganese coin cell; don't use lithium-ion or non-rechargeable cells.

Q: How do I enable charging for the RTC battery on my Pi 5?
A: Add `dtparam=rtc_bbat_vchg=3000000` to `/boot/firmware/config.txt` and reboot.

# Serial peripheral interface (SPI)

Q: How do I enable SPI on my Raspberry Pi?
A: Use raspi-config or add `dtparam=spi=on` to `/boot/firmware/config.txt`.

Q: How many SPI buses does the Raspberry Pi 4 have?
A: Pi 4 has SPI0 through SPI6, giving seven SPI buses total.

Q: What pins does SPI0 use on the standard 40-pin header?
A: MOSI on GPIO10, MISO on GPIO9, SCLK on GPIO11, CE0 on GPIO8, CE1 on GPIO7.

Q: Can I use Python to talk to SPI devices on my Pi?
A: Yes, libraries like `spidev` and `SPI-Py` provide Python access to SPI.

# Universal Serial Bus (USB)

Q: My USB hard drive isn't working reliably connected directly to my Pi. What should I do?
A: Use a powered USB hub, as hard drives typically require more power than the Pi provides.

Q: I plugged a USB 3.0 hub into my Pi 3 and my mouse stopped working. Why?
A: There's a known bug where pre-Pi 4 models can't communicate with low/full-speed devices through USB 3.0 hubs.

Q: How much total USB power can a Raspberry Pi 4 provide to connected devices?
A: Pi 4 provides up to 1200mA total across all USB ports combined.

# Raspberry Pi revision codes

Q: How do I find the revision code for my Raspberry Pi?
A: Run `cat /proc/cpuinfo` and look at the Revision line at the bottom.

Q: I'm writing a program to detect which Pi model I have. Should I just compare revision codes?
A: No, use the board-type or memory fields instead; new revisions create new codes that break lists.

Q: How do I check what Raspberry Pi model I have in a way that works across Linux distros?
A: Run `cat /proc/device-tree/compatible | tr '\0' '\n'` to see board and CPU strings.

Q: What revision code would tell me I have a Raspberry Pi 5 with 8 GB RAM?
A: The revision code `d04170` indicates a Raspberry Pi 5 revision 1.0 with 8 GB RAM.
