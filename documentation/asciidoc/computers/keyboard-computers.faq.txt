# Whole Document

Q: What's the difference between the Raspberry Pi 400, 500, and 500+?
A: They differ in processor, RAM, GPU, storage, keys, and extra features.

Q: I want a keyboard computer but don't know which one to get. What are my options?
A: Raspberry Pi offers three keyboard computers: the 400, 500, and 500+.

Q: Do these keyboard computers come with everything I need to get started?
A: They're available standalone or as kits including mouse, cable, and microSD card.

Q: Can I get these keyboard computers in different languages?
A: Yes, they come in regional variants affecting keyboard layout and power supply.

# Features

Q: How much faster is the 500 compared to the 400?
A: The 500 uses a Cortex-A76 at 2.4 GHz versus the 400's Cortex-A72 at 1.8 GHz.

Q: I do a lot of multitasking. Which keyboard computer has the most RAM?
A: The Raspberry Pi 500+ has the most RAM at 16 GB.

Q: Does the 400 support HDR video output?
A: No, only the Raspberry Pi 500 and 500+ support HDR on HDMI outputs.

Q: How do I turn off the Raspberry Pi 400 if there's no power button?
A: Press Fn plus F10 to turn it on and off instead of a dedicated button.

Q: What's special about the 500+ compared to the regular 500?
A: The 500+ adds mechanical keys, RGB backlighting, replaceable keycaps, and 256 GB SSD.

Q: Do all three keyboard computers have the same dimensions?
A: No, the 500+ is larger at 312 by 123 by 35 mm versus 286 by 122 by 23 mm.

Q: What kind of storage does the 500+ have that the others don't?
A: The 500+ includes an internal 256 GB M.2 SSD alongside the microSD card slot.

Q: How many USB ports do these keyboard computers have?
A: They each have two USB 3.0 ports and one USB 2.0 port.

Q: Do any of these support wireless connectivity?
A: All three support dual-band Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Low Energy.

Q: How much power does the Raspberry Pi 400 need compared to the 500?
A: The 400 needs 5V at 3A (15W); the 500 and 500+ need 5V at 5A (25W).

Q: Can I remap keys on the Raspberry Pi 400?
A: Key remapping is only available on the Raspberry Pi 500 and 500+, not the 400.

# Keyboard computer kits

Q: What's included in the Raspberry Pi 500 kit?
A: It includes the keyboard, 27W power supply, mouse, 32 GB microSD card, cable, and book.

Q: Does the 400 kit come with a microSD card that already has the OS on it?
A: Yes, the 400 kit includes a 16 GB microSD card pre-programmed with Raspberry Pi OS.

Q: The 500+ kit doesn't seem to include a microSD card. Where does the OS come from?
A: The 500+ includes an SSD pre-programmed with Raspberry Pi OS instead of a microSD card.

Q: What's the spudger that comes with the 500+ kit for?
A: It's a plastic opening tool for accessing and replacing the internal M.2 SSD.

Q: Does the 500 kit include a bigger microSD card than the 400 kit?
A: Yes, the 500 kit includes 32 GB versus the 400 kit's 16 GB microSD card.

Q: I bought the 500 kit with the pre-loaded microSD card. Do I need to do the full setup?

# Raspberry Pi 500+ mechanical keys

Q: What kind of switches does the 500+ use under its keys?
A: It uses Gateron KS-33 low-profile mechanical switches with a custom grey stem.

Q: Are the keycaps on the 500+ compatible with standard third-party keycap sets?
A: Yes, they use Cherry MX-compatible cross-shaped stems, which is the industry standard.

Q: What type of switches are on the 500+ — clicky, tactile, or linear?
A: The 500+ uses clicky Gateron KS-33 switches with a custom grey stem.

# Raspberry Pi 500+ customisation

Q: What can I actually customise on the 500+?
A: You can replace the SSD, swap keycaps, and adjust backlighting presets, colours, and brightness.

Q: Can I remap keys on the fly on the 500+?
A: Yes, advanced users can do on-the-fly key remapping on the Raspberry Pi 500+.

# Replace your M.2 SSD

Q: Can I put a bigger SSD in my 500+ if I run out of storage?
A: Yes, you can replace the M.2 SSD to expand storage capacity.

Q: What M.2 form factors does the 500+ support?
A: It supports M.2 M key edge connectors in form factors 2230, 2242, 2260, and 2280.

Q: What tools do I need to swap out the SSD in my 500+?
A: You need a cross head screwdriver, flat head screwdriver, and the provided plastic spudger.

Q: I'm replacing the SSD but worried about breaking the keyboard apart. Any tips?
A: Fold the top over rather than lifting it; both halves are connected by a flexible cable.

Q: Can I use the M.2 slot for something other than an SSD?
A: Yes, you can also use that slot to install a different PCIe peripheral.

# Change your keycaps

Q: Can I put any third-party keycaps on the 500+?
A: Most Cherry MX-compatible third-party keycaps work, but avoid taller profiles.

Q: How do I actually pull a keycap off the 500+?
A: Insert the keycap puller prongs on opposite sides, grip, then gently pull straight up.

Q: Can I take off the keycaps just to clean under them?
A: Yes, you can remove keycaps to clean underneath them.

Q: What's the right way to put a keycap back on?
A: Align the cross-shaped stems and press the keycap down firmly until fully seated.

# Keyboard layout

Q: I want to buy a replacement keycap set. Which language layouts are easiest to find?
A: US layouts are most widely available, followed by UK layouts.

Q: Are all the keys on the 500+ standard so I can replace them easily?
A: No, several keys like the power key, SysRq, Ins, and function labels are non-standard.

Q: Will a third-party keycap set include the Raspberry Pi logo key?
A: No, third-party sets typically say Win instead and don't include the Raspberry Pi logo.

# Keycap profiles

Q: What keycap profile should I get for my 500+?
A: DSA or Cherry profiles are recommended; XDA, OEM, and SA are too tall.

Q: Why can't I use SA keycaps on the 500+?
A: SA keycaps are taller and can hit the keyboard frame or produce too much noise.

Q: What's the difference between DSA and Cherry keycap profiles?
A: DSA is flat and swappable across rows; Cherry is sculpted with varying row heights.

# Lighting

Q: Will fully opaque keycaps block the backlighting on the 500+?
A: Yes, the LEDs light the top half of keycaps, so fully opaque caps might block illumination.

# Customise your backlighting

Q: My 500+ keyboard seems to have no lighting. Is that normal?
A: Yes, most backlighting is off by default; only the power LED and Caps Lock are enabled.

Q: What does the little light behind the power button mean?
A: It's red when powered but off, green when on, and blinks with SD card activity.

Q: How do I cycle through the different lighting presets?
A: Press Fn plus F4 to cycle forwards, or Fn plus Shift plus F4 to cycle backwards.

Q: How many lighting presets does the 500+ come with?
A: It comes with seven presets, numbered 0 through 6.

Q: What's the Typing Heatmap preset?
A: The more you press a key, the redder it gets, and surrounding keys light up too.

Q: How do I adjust the brightness of the keyboard backlight?
A: Press Fn plus F5 to reduce brightness or Fn plus F6 to increase brightness.

Q: How do I change the colour of the backlight?
A: Press Fn plus F3 to cycle forwards through 16 colours, or add Shift to go backwards.

Q: I want the keyboard to light up when I press keys. Which preset does that?
A: Preset 6, Solid Reactive Simple, lights up keys when you press them.

# Advanced software settings

Q: I want to do more advanced LED stuff on my 500+. What do I need to install?
A: Install rpi-keyboard-fw-update and rpi-keyboard-config using the commands provided.

Q: How do I update the keyboard firmware before customising things?
A: Run sudo apt install rpi-keyboard-fw-update then sudo rpi-keyboard-fw-update.

Q: Wait, there's actually a game on the 500+ keyboard? How do I play it?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config game; it uses the LEDs as a screen to play Flappy Bird.

Q: How do I see a list of all the commands available in the keyboard config tool?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config help to list all available commands with brief descriptions.

# Advanced LED settings

Q: Can I control individual LEDs on my 500+ keyboard?
A: Yes, configure the direct effect to set individual LED colours by row and column.

Q: How do I set a specific LED to a particular colour?
A: Use rpi-keyboard-config led set with the row and column coordinates and colour value.

Q: I messed up my presets. How do I reset them back to default?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config reset-presets to restore default presets and clear LED customisations.

Q: Can I save my custom LED layout and reload it later?
A: Yes, use rpi-keyboard-config leds save and then leds load to apply it later.

Q: What values can I use for speed, saturation, and hue parameters?
A: Each parameter takes a number between 0 and 255.

Q: I want no startup animation on my keyboard. How do I turn it off?
A: Set the startup animation to START_ANIM_NONE for that preset using the config software.

Q: How many different startup animations can I choose from?
A: There are five startup animation options available for each preset.

# Remap keys on your keyboard

Q: I want to remap my keys. How do I find out what keycode to use?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config list-keycodes to see all possible keycodes.

Q: How do I remap a specific key on the 500+?
A: Use rpi-keyboard-config key set with the row, column, and the keycode you want.

Q: How do I figure out the row and column number for a specific key?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config info --ascii to see a layout diagram with coordinates.

Q: Can I monitor which keys I'm pressing using the config tool?
A: Yes, run rpi-keyboard-config key watch after unlocking the keyboard first.

Q: How do I unlock the keyboard before watching key presses?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config unlock and hold the specified keys until the countdown completes.

Q: I've set up different layers for my keys. How many layers can I have?
A: You can edit up to four layers, numbered 0 through 3.

Q: Can I use a graphical tool instead of the command line to remap keys?
A: Yes, the Raspberry Pi 500 and 500+ are compatible with the Vial web interface.

Q: How do I reset my keyboard layout back to the default if I've messed it up?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config reset-keymap to restore all default key mappings and layers.

# Return to default settings

Q: I messed up all my settings. How do I reset everything back to factory defaults?
A: Run sudo rpi-keyboard-fw-update -w -i to wipe all customisations and restore defaults.

Q: My 500+ keyboard is so messed up I can't type anything. Can I still reset it?
A: Yes, connect an external USB keyboard and enter the reset command from that device.

Q: Will my custom settings survive a reboot or firmware update?
A: Yes, software-controlled preferences are saved in flash memory and persist through both.

# Flappy Bird game

Q: How does the Flappy Bird game work on the 500+ keyboard?
A: The keyboard backlighting acts as the screen, with lights representing the bird and pipes.

Q: How do I control the bird in the keyboard Flappy Bird game?
A: Press the space bar to make the bird fly over the pipe obstacles.

Q: How do I know what my score is in the keyboard game?
A: The number of purple LEDs along the top of the keyboard shows your score.

Q: How do I quit the Flappy Bird game on the keyboard?
A: Press Q on your keyboard to exit the game.
