# Attach a Camera Module

Q: What are the two camera interfaces on the Compute Module called?
A: The Compute Module has two CSI-2 camera interfaces called CAM1 and CAM0.

Q: I want to hook up a camera to my Compute Module. What board do I need for that?
A: You can connect cameras using a Compute Module I/O Board with CAM1 and CAM0 interfaces.

# Update your system

Q: Do I need to update anything before I set up a camera on my Compute Module?
A: Yes, update your firmware first by running sudo apt update and sudo apt full-upgrade.

Q: What's the command to make sure my Pi firmware is up to date before setting up the camera?
A: Run sudo apt update followed by sudo apt full-upgrade to update your system.

# Connect one camera

Q: I've got a single camera and want to connect it to my Compute Module. Where do I plug it in?
A: Connect the Camera Module to the CAM1 port using an RPI-CAMERA board or Zero camera cable.

Q: Do I need to set jumpers on the CM5 when connecting a camera?
A: Yes, on CM5 only, fit two jumpers on J6 per the board's written instructions.

Q: Which GPIO pins do I need to connect with jumper cables for a camera on a CM4S?
A: Connect GPIO 0 to CD1_SDA, 1 to CD1_SCL, 2 to CAM1_I01, and 3 to CAM1_I00.

Q: I'm setting up a camera and there's a camera_auto_detect line in my config. Should I leave it?
A: No, remove or comment out camera_auto_detect=1 and dtparam=i2c_arm=on from config.txt.

Q: I've got a CM3 and I'm connecting a camera — do I need any special overlay in config.txt?
A: Yes, add dtoverlay=cm-swap-i2c0 to accommodate the swapped GPIO pin assignment on the I/O board.

Q: What do I put in config.txt for a v3 Raspberry Pi camera?
A: Add dtoverlay=imx708 to /boot/firmware/config.txt for the v3 camera.

Q: I've got the HQ camera. What's the directive I need in config.txt?
A: Add dtoverlay=imx477 to /boot/firmware/config.txt for the HQ camera.

Q: How do I check if my camera was detected properly after setting everything up?
A: Run rpicam-hello --list to see a list of detected cameras.

Q: What's the overlay for the GS camera in config.txt?
A: Add dtoverlay=imx296 to /boot/firmware/config.txt for the GS camera.

Q: I've got an older v1 camera module. What line do I add to config.txt?
A: Add dtoverlay=ov5647 to /boot/firmware/config.txt for the v1 camera.

# Connect two cameras

Q: Can I connect two cameras to my Compute Module at the same time?
A: Yes, connect one camera to CAM1 and a second to CAM0 using an RPI-CAMERA board or Zero cable.

Q: I'm adding a second camera to my CM4. Do I need to do something with J6?
A: Yes, on CM4 connect the J6 GPIO pins with two vertical-orientation jumpers.

Q: What GPIO pins do I wire up for the second camera on a CM3 plus?
A: Connect GPIO 28 to CD0_SDA, 29 to CD0_SCL, 30 to CAM0_I01, and 31 to CAM0_I00.

Q: What's the config.txt directive for a v2 camera connected to the CAM0 port?
A: Add dtoverlay=imx219,cam0 to /boot/firmware/config.txt for a v2 camera on CAM0.

Q: How do I verify both cameras show up after connecting them?
A: Run rpicam-hello --list and you should see both camera models in the output.

# Take a picture

Q: What's the command to just snap a photo with my camera module?
A: Run rpicam-still --datetime -e png to take a picture saved as a PNG file.

Q: Can I set the resolution when taking a photo from the command line?
A: Yes, use the --width and --height options to specify image dimensions.

Q: I want to add a delay before the photo is taken. How do I do that?
A: Use the -t option followed by a delay value in milliseconds.

# Take a video

Q: How do I record a video with my camera module from the terminal?
A: Run rpicam-vid -t 10000 -o video.h264 to record a ten-second h264 video.

Q: What format does rpicam-vid save videos in by default?
A: Videos are saved using the h264 codec, as shown in the example output file video.h264.

# Specify which camera to use

Q: I've got two cameras connected. How do I tell my app which one to use?
A: Use the --camera option with an index number, like rpicam-hello --camera 0 or --camera 1.

Q: How do I find out what index number each of my cameras has?
A: Run rpicam-hello --list-cameras to see each camera listed with its index number.

Q: My Compute Module has two cameras. Which one gets used if I don't specify?
A: By default libcamera uses the camera with index 0 in the --list-cameras list.

# I2C mapping of GPIO pins

Q: What GPIO pins does the CM4 I/O Board use for i2c-10?
A: The CM4 I/O Board maps i2c-10 to GPIOs 44 and 45.

Q: I'm using a CM3 I/O Board — which pins are mapped to i2c-0?
A: On CM1, CM3, CM3+, and CM4S I/O Boards, i2c-0 is mapped to GPIOs 28 and 29.

Q: Why do I need the cm-swap-i2c0 overlay for older Compute Modules?
A: It accommodates the swapped GPIO pin assignment on CM1, CM3, CM3+, and CM4S I/O Boards.

Q: I've got a custom board with a different pin layout for I2C. Is there a way to handle that?
A: Yes, use alternate overrides like i2c0-gpio0 or i2c10-gpio44 depending on your board layout.

# GPIO pins for shutdown

Q: How does camera shutdown work on the CM4 when I've got two cameras?
A: The CM4 IO board provides one GPIO pin shared by both cameras as a single regulator.

Q: I'm on a CM3 and the camera regulators seem disabled. Is that normal?
A: Yes, CM1, CM3, CM3+, and CM4S I/O boards provide no GPIO pin for cam1_reg or cam0_reg by default.

Q: How do I enable the camera regulators on a CM3 board?
A: Add dtparam=cam1_reg and dtparam=cam0_reg to /boot/firmware/config.txt to enable them.

Q: I want to assign the CAM1 regulator to a specific pin on my custom board. How do I do that?
A: Add dtparam=cam1_reg_gpio=<pin number> to /boot/firmware/config.txt with your chosen pin.

Q: Do these camera regulator GPIO directives work with expander pins?
A: No, these directives only work for GPIO pins connected directly to the SoC.

# Whole Document

Q: I just got a Compute Module and want to use a camera. What software does Raspberry Pi OS include for that?
A: Raspberry Pi OS includes the libcamera library to help you take images with your camera.

Q: I've got a CM5 and a CM4 — do they need different steps when connecting cameras?
A: Yes, CM5 needs jumpers on J6, while CM4 and older boards require specific GPIO jumper cable connections.

Q: What cable do I need to connect a camera module to the Compute Module I/O Board?
A: Use an RPI-CAMERA board or a Raspberry Pi Zero camera cable to connect the camera.

Q: Do I need to reboot after changing config.txt for the camera settings?
A: Yes, reboot your Compute Module with sudo reboot after editing config.txt.
