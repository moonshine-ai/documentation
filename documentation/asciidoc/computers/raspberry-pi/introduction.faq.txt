# Introduction

Q: So what's the difference between all the different Raspberry Pi series? There are so many of them.
A: There are five series: flagship SBCs, keyboard computers, Zero, Compute Modules, and Pico microcontrollers.

Q: Does every Raspberry Pi run Linux?
A: No, Pico microcontroller boards don't run Linux; they're for embedded projects.

Q: I want something tiny and cheap that still runs Linux. What should I look at?
A: The Zero series offers small, affordable boards with full Linux and low power consumption.

Q: What's a Compute Module? Is it just a normal Pi?
A: Compute Modules are for embedded and industrial use, with no on-board connectors, needing a baseboard.

Q: What's the difference between a Pico and a regular Pi?
A: Picos are microcontrollers without Linux; programs flash directly to on-board memory instead.

# Flagship single-board computer (SBC) series

Q: What does Model B actually mean on a Raspberry Pi?
A: Model B means the board has an Ethernet port; Model A is smaller with no Ethernet.

Q: I've got an old Raspberry Pi 2 Model B — is it still supported?
A: Revisions 1.1 and 1.2 reached End-of-Life on 16 October 2025 due to discontinued SoC.

Q: How many USB ports does the Pi 4 have?
A: The Pi 4 has two USB 2.0 and two USB 3.0 ports.

Q: Does the Raspberry Pi 5 have a headphone jack?
A: Not specified in this section.

Q: How much RAM can I get in a Raspberry Pi 5?
A: Raspberry Pi 5 is available with up to 16 GB of memory.

Q: Does the Pi 3 B+ have Wi-Fi? I need both 2.4 and 5 GHz.
A: Yes, the Pi 3 B+ has dual-band 2.4 and 5 GHz 802.11ac Wi-Fi at 100 Mb/s.

Q: I need to connect a camera to my Pi. Does the original Model B have a camera port?
A: Yes, the original Model B has a standard 15-pin CSI camera port.

Q: What kind of power connector does the Pi 4 use?
A: The Pi 4 uses USB-C power at 5 V and 3 A, which is 15 watts.

Q: Does the Pi 5 support PoE?
A: Yes, the Pi 5 has Gigabit Ethernet with PoE+ support.

Q: I want to add an SSD or something via PCIe to my Pi 5. Is there a connector for that?
A: Yes, the Raspberry Pi 5 has a single-lane PCIe FFC connector.

Q: How many HDMI ports does the Raspberry Pi 4 have?
A: The Pi 4 has two micro-HDMI ports.

Q: What's the difference between the Pi 4 and Pi 5 in terms of Wi-Fi speed?
A: Pi 4 gets 120 Mb/s Wi-Fi; Pi 5 gets Wi-Fi 5 at 300 Mb/s.

# Keyboard computer (X00) series

Q: So the Raspberry Pi 400, is that basically just a Pi 4 inside a keyboard?
A: Yes, the 400 uses the BCM2711 SoC, same as the Pi 4, inside a keyboard case.

Q: How much RAM does the Pi 500 have?
A: The Raspberry Pi 500 has 8 GB of memory.

Q: Does the Pi 400 have a headphone jack?
A: Not specified in this section.

Q: What makes the Pi 500+ different from the regular 500?
A: The 500+ has 16 GB RAM, an internal 256 GB M.2 SSD, and higher power delivery.

Q: Does the Pi 400 have USB 3 ports?
A: Yes, the Pi 400 has two USB 3.0 ports plus one USB 2.0 port.

# Zero series

Q: What does the W mean in Raspberry Pi Zero W?
A: The W suffix means the board includes wireless connectivity like Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.

Q: I bought a Pi Zero and it doesn't have any pins. Do I need to solder them myself?
A: Yes, models without the H suffix require you to solder pins manually or use a third-party kit.

Q: Does the Pi Zero have a full-size HDMI port?
A: No, all Zero models use a mini HDMI port, not a full-size one.

Q: Can I connect a camera to a Raspberry Pi Zero?
A: Yes, since version 1.3 all Zero models include a mini 22-pin CSI camera port.

Q: What's the difference between the Zero WH and the Zero W?
A: The WH comes with header pins pre-soldered; the W has an unpopulated GPIO header.

Q: How much RAM does the Pi Zero 2 W have?
A: The Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W has 512 MB of memory.

Q: Does any Pi Zero model have Ethernet built in?
A: Not specified in this section.

# Compute Module series

Q: I'm building something into a product. Which Compute Module should I use for a new design?
A: For new non-SODIMM designs, Compute Module 4 or Compute Module 5 are recommended.

Q: Is the Compute Module 3 still available or has it been discontinued?
A: CM3 and CM3 Lite reached End-of-Life on 16 October 2025 due to discontinued SoC.

Q: Does the Compute Module have Wi-Fi built in?
A: Compute Module 4 and 5 offer optional Wi-Fi; earlier SODIMM modules have none.

Q: I see Compute Modules use a DDR2 SODIMM shape. Can I just plug them into a regular laptop slot?
A: No, Compute Modules using the DDR2 SODIMM form factor are not electrically compatible with DDR2 SODIMM.

Q: How much storage can I get on a Compute Module 5?
A: Compute Module 5 is available with up to 64 GB of storage, or zero in the Lite version.

# Pico microcontroller boards

Q: What does the H mean on a Raspberry Pi Pico H?
A: The H suffix means header pins are pre-soldered onto the GPIO header.

Q: Does the Pico W support Bluetooth?
A: Yes, the Pico W supports Bluetooth 5.2 and Bluetooth Low Energy.

Q: What's the difference between the Pico and the Pico 2?
A: The Pico 2 uses the RP2350 chip and has 520 kB RAM and 4 MB storage versus the original's specs.

Q: I need Wi-Fi on my Pico project. Which one do I buy?
A: Buy any Pico model with the W suffix, like Pico W or Pico 2 W, for 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi.

Q: How do I put code on a Pico? Do I use a microSD card?
A: No, programs are flashed directly onto the Pico's on-board memory, not removable storage.

Q: How much RAM does the original Pico have? Is it enough for most projects?
A: The original Pico has 264 kB of RAM; whether that's enough depends on your project.

# Whole Document

Q: I just want a basic Pi to learn on. Which series should I even be looking at?
A: The flagship SBC series offers high performance, full Linux, and common ports for general use.

Q: What's the most RAM I can get on any Raspberry Pi right now?
A: The Raspberry Pi 5 and Pi 500+ both offer up to 16 GB of memory.

Q: Can any Pi do Bluetooth? I want to connect a wireless controller.
A: Yes, many Pi models including Pi 3, Pi 4, Pi 5, Zero W, and Pico W support Bluetooth.

Q: I want something with built-in storage, not just an SD card. Which Pi has that?
A: The Raspberry Pi 500+ has an internal 256 GB M.2 SSD built in.
