# Universal Serial Bus (USB)

Q: Can I use pretty much any USB device with my Raspberry Pi?
A: Generally yes, every Linux-supported device works, with some pre-Pi-4 limitations.

Q: My USB device keeps acting weird. Could it be a power problem?
A: Yes, power issues often cause USB problems; try a powered hub to rule it out.

# Maximum power output

Q: How much power can the USB ports on a Pi 4 give out total?
A: Raspberry Pi 2, 3, and 4 provide 1200mA total across all USB ports.

Q: I've got a Pi Zero — how much power does its USB port supply?
A: Raspberry Pi Zero USB ports supply a maximum of 500mA per port.

Q: What's different about the power on a Pi 5 depending on which power supply I use?
A: Pi 5 gives 600mA with a 3A supply, or 1600mA with a 5A supply.

# Raspberry Pi 5

Q: What kind of power supply does the Pi 5 actually need to boot?
A: It needs a USB-C supply capable of delivering 3A at 5V, which is 15W.

Q: I plugged in my Pi 5 and it's warning me about restricted power to peripherals. What does that mean?
A: Your supply can't provide 5A at 5V, so peripheral current draw is limited to 600mA.

Q: I want to run a hard drive off my Pi 5. What power supply should I get?
A: Use a USB-PD supply capable of 5A at 5V, which is 25W.

Q: Does the fan on the Pi 5 share power with the USB ports?
A: Yes, the power budget is shared between USB ports and the fan header.

Q: If my Pi 5 detects a 25W power supply, does it unlock more USB power?
A: Yes, firmware increases USB peripheral current limit to 1.6A with a 5A supply detected.

# Raspberry Pi 4

Q: How many USB 3.0 ports does the Pi 4 have?
A: Raspberry Pi 4 has two USB 3.0 ports and two USB 2.0 ports.

Q: Does the Pi 4 have any USB limitations I should know about?
A: All four ports share a single USB 2.0 hub internally, limiting USB 1.1 and 2.0 bandwidth.

# Raspberry Pi Zero, 1, 2 and 3

Q: How many USB ports does the Pi 3 have?
A: Raspberry Pi 1 Model B+, 2, and 3 boards all offer four USB 2.0 ports.

Q: What kind of USB port does the Pi Zero have?
A: The Pi Zero has one micro USB on-the-go, or OTG, port.

Q: Is there a speed limit on USB for older Pi models?
A: Yes, older Pi models have a single USB bus operating at a maximum of 480Mbps.

Q: I've got a webcam and a keyboard plugged into my Pi 3. Are those different USB speeds?
A: Keyboards are low speed; webcams are typically high speed devices per USB 2.0 spec.

Q: Can I plug lots of high-speed USB devices into an older Pi without problems?
A: Generally there are no issues connecting multiple high-speed USB devices to a Raspberry Pi.

# Known USB issues

## Interoperability with USB 3.0 hubs

Q: I plugged my keyboard into a USB 3.0 hub connected to my Pi and it's not working. What's going on?
A: A bug in most USB 3.0 hub hardware prevents older Pi models from talking to low-speed devices.

Q: My mouse doesn't work through my USB 3.0 hub on my Pi 3. Is there a fix?
A: Plug a USB 2.0 hub between the Pi and USB 3.0 hub, then connect your mouse there.

Q: Does a USB 2.0 hub work fine when plugged into a USB 3.0 hub on an older Pi?
A: Yes, USB 2.0 high-speed devices including USB 2.0 hubs work correctly via a USB 3.0 hub.

## USB 1.1 webcams

Q: My old webcam is really unreliable on my Pi. Is there anything I can do?
A: Try using the camera at a lower resolution to reduce data transfer demands.

Q: Why does my older webcam have trouble working reliably on a Pi?
A: Old webcams can be full-speed devices, causing high data transfer and extra software overhead.

## Esoteric USB sound cards

Q: I've got a fancy high-end USB DAC and it's not working reliably on my Pi. Any ideas?
A: Force the output to CD quality, 44.1 or 48 kHz 16-bit, to reduce USB bandwidth usage.

Q: Will my 192 kHz USB sound card work reliably on a Raspberry Pi?
A: Reliable operation with 96kHz or 192kHz DACs is not guaranteed on Raspberry Pi.

## Single TT USB hubs

Q: What's the difference between a single TT and multi TT USB hub?
A: Single TT has one translator for all ports; multi TT has one translator per port.

Q: I've got lots of low-speed devices and my single TT hub is acting up. What should I do?
A: Use a multi TT hub, or spread low-speed devices between the Pi's own ports and the hub.

Q: My USB hub has some devices behaving unreliably. Could the hub type be the problem?
A: Yes, too many low or full-speed devices on a single TT hub can cause unreliable behavior.
