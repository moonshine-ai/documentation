# Power Supply

Q: What voltage do all Raspberry Pis need?
A: All Raspberry Pi models require a 5.1V power supply to operate.

Q: My Pi 4 needs a new power supply. What connector type does it use?
A: Raspberry Pi 4, Pi 400, and Pi 5 all use a USB-C connector.

Q: I've got an older Pi 3. Does it use the same power connector as the Pi 4?
A: No, Pi 3 and earlier models use a micro USB power connector instead.

# Recommended Power Supplies

Q: What power supply should I get for my Raspberry Pi 5?
A: Raspberry Pi recommends the 27W USB-C Power Supply for the Pi 5.

Q: I've got a Pi 4. What's the recommended power supply for it?
A: Raspberry Pi recommends the 3A USB-C Supply made for the Pi 4.

Q: Does the Raspberry Pi support USB-PPS charging?
A: No, no Raspberry Pi models support USB-PPS.

Q: I'm using a multi-port USB-PD charger and my Pi randomly booted when it was off. What happened?
A: Plugging another device into a shared USB-PD supply can trigger renegotiation, potentially booting a powered-down Pi.

# Power over Ethernet (PoE) Connector

Q: Can I power my Pi 5 through the ethernet cable?
A: Yes, the Pi 5 Ethernet jack supports PoE+ using the IEEE 802.3at-2009 standard.

Q: My Pi 4B has an ethernet port. Can I power it over ethernet?
A: Yes, Pi 4B supports PoE using the IEEE 802.3af-2003 standard, but needs a PoE HAT.

Q: Do I need any extra hardware to use PoE on my Pi?
A: Yes, all PoE-capable Pi models need a HAT to actually draw power through Ethernet.

Q: Which PoE HAT should I get for a Pi 5?
A: For Pi 5, which supports PoE+, Raspberry Pi recommends the PoE+ HAT.

# Typical Power Requirements

Q: How much power does a Raspberry Pi Zero use?
A: The Pi Zero draws about 100mA at idle and needs a 1.2A power supply.

Q: I'm connecting a bunch of USB devices to my Pi 4. How much current can it give them?
A: Pi 4 can provide up to 1.2A total to downstream USB peripherals.

Q: My Pi 5 is only giving 600mA to USB devices. Is that a problem with my Pi?
A: No, Pi 5 limits USB power to 600mA unless your power supply provides 5A at 5V.

Q: How do I check how much power my Pi is giving to its USB ports?
A: Run the command `vcgencmd get_config usb_max_current_enable` in the terminal.

Q: I want to connect an external hard drive to my Pi but I'm worried it won't get enough power. What should I do?
A: Use an externally-powered USB hub for devices that need more current than the Pi provides.

Q: How much current can I safely pull from all the GPIO pins together?
A: All GPIO pins combined can safely draw 50mA, with each individual pin up to 16mA.

Q: How much power does the HDMI port use on a Pi?
A: The HDMI port consumes 50mA of power.

Q: My camera module isn't working reliably. Could it be a power issue?
A: Possibly — the Camera Module requires 250mA, so check your power supply is adequate.

# Decrease Raspberry Pi 5 Wattage When Turned Off

Q: My Pi 5 is still using a lot of power even when it's turned off. Is there a way to reduce that?
A: Yes, edit the EEPROM config with `sudo rpi-eeprom-config -e` and set POWER_OFF_ON_HALT=1.

Q: How much power does a Pi 5 use when it's shut down normally?
A: By default, a powered-down Pi 5 consumes around 1W to 1.4W.

Q: If I change the EEPROM settings to reduce idle power on my Pi 5, how low can it go?
A: With the right EEPROM settings, power consumption when off drops to around 0.01W.

# Power Supply Warnings

Q: I'm seeing low voltage warnings in my Pi's logs. What does that mean?
A: It means your supply voltage dropped below 4.63V, and you should switch to a better power supply.

Q: Can a bad power supply actually damage my files?
A: Yes, low quality power supplies can corrupt storage or cause unpredictable behaviour.

Q: Why would my Pi's voltage keep dropping? I haven't changed anything.
A: Too many high-demand USB devices, an inadequate supply, or thin power cable wires can cause drops.

Q: Which Pi models have low voltage detection built in?
A: All Pi models since the B+ in 2014 have it, except the Zero range.

# Power Supplies and Raspberry Pi OS

Q: How can I check what the PMIC is actually measuring on my Pi?
A: Run `vcgencmd pmic_read_adc` to view ADC measurements from the PMIC.

Q: Can I monitor the core voltage on my Pi?
A: Yes, using `vcgencmd pmic_read_adc` can show useful measurements like core voltage.

Q: Why doesn't the PMIC reading add up to the total wattage of my power supply?
A: USB current and anything connected directly to 5V bypasses the PMIC, so it won't show up.

# Back-Powering

Q: What's back-powering and should I be worried about it?
A: Back-powering is when a USB hub supplies power back to your Pi, bypassing its protection circuitry.

Q: I plugged in a powered USB hub and now my Pi seems to be getting power from it. Is that okay?
A: No, back-powering bypasses Pi's protection circuits, leaving it vulnerable to damage from power surges.

# Whole Document

Q: I just bought a Pi 5. What power supply, connector type, and current does it need?
A: Pi 5 needs a 27W USB-C supply with 5A capacity and uses a USB-C connector.

Q: Does it matter if I use a cheap power supply with my Pi?
A: Yes, low quality supplies can corrupt storage and cause unpredictable behaviour on your Pi.

Q: I've got a Pi 3B and a Pi 4. Do they need different power supplies?
A: Yes, Pi 3 needs a 2.5A micro USB supply and Pi 4 needs a 3A USB-C supply.
