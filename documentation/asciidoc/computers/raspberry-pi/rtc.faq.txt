# Real Time Clock (RTC)

Q: Does the Raspberry Pi 5 have a built-in clock that keeps time?
A: Yes, the Raspberry Pi 5 includes a built-in RTC module.

Q: Where's the battery connector for the clock on the Pi 5? I can't find it.
A: It's near the left corner, right of the USB-C power connector, labeled J5.

Q: My Pi doesn't have internet access so it can't get the time from NTP. Is there another way?
A: The RTC provides the time on boot, visible in dmesg, without NTP access.

Q: I want my Pi to wake up automatically at a certain time. Is that something it can do?
A: Yes, you can set a wake alarm that powers the board back on at a set time.

Q: How much power does it use when it's in that low-power sleep mode waiting for the alarm?
A: The board uses approximately 3 mA in the low-power wake alarm state.

Q: I want to test the wake alarm thing. What do I actually do?
A: Run the echo plus 600 command, then sudo halt; it wakes after ten minutes.

Q: Do I need to mess with the bootloader to get the wake alarm working?
A: Yes, edit bootloader config to add POWER_OFF_ON_HALT equals 1 and WAKE_ON_GPIO equals 0.

Q: I don't have a battery plugged into the J5 connector. Can I still use the RTC?
A: Yes, the RTC is still usable even without a backup battery attached.

# Add a backup battery

Q: What kind of battery does the Pi 5 RTC use?
A: It uses a rechargeable lithium manganese coin cell with a two-pin JST-SH plug.

Q: How long will the RTC battery last before it dies?
A: With single-digit µA draw when powered down, retention time measures in months.

Q: Can I just use a regular non-rechargeable lithium battery for the RTC?
A: No, non-rechargeable lithium cells aren't recommended due to shorter service life.

Q: What about using a lithium ion battery instead? Would that work?
A: No, don't use a lithium ion cell for the RTC under any circumstances.

# Enable battery charging

Q: I plugged in the RTC battery but it's not charging. Is charging on by default?
A: No, battery charging is disabled by default and must be enabled manually.

Q: How do I actually turn on charging for the RTC battery?
A: Add dtparam equals rtc_bbat_vchg equals your voltage value to config.txt, then reboot.

Q: How do I check if the charging voltage got set correctly after I rebooted?
A: Check the sysfs files under sys/class/rtc/rtc0 to verify the charging voltage.

Q: What's the charging current for the RTC battery?
A: The RTC charger uses a constant current of 3 mA.

# Disable battery charging

Q: I want to stop the RTC battery from charging. How do I turn that off?
A: Remove any lines containing rtc_bbat_vchg from your config.txt file.

Q: So I've got a Pi 5 and I want it to automatically wake up every hour to take a photo. Is that doable?
A: Yes, the wake alarm feature is described as useful for periodic jobs like time-lapse imagery.

Q: What's the difference between the RTC on the Pi 5 and just getting the time from the internet?
A: The RTC provides time on boot locally, useful when NTP internet access isn't available.
