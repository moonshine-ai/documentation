# Getting started with your Raspberry Pi

Q: I just got a Raspberry Pi, what do I actually need to get it running?
A: You need a power supply and boot media like a microSD card.

Q: What's a headless setup? Do I need a monitor for that?
A: Headless means network-only access, so no monitor or peripherals needed.

Q: I don't want to plug in a keyboard or screen. Can I still use it?
A: Yes, preconfigure hostname, user, network, and SSH during OS installation.

# Power supply

Q: What power supply does a Raspberry Pi 5 need?
A: It needs a 5V/5A supply, like the official 27W USB-C power supply.

Q: I've got a Pi 4, what charger should I be using for it?
A: Raspberry Pi 4 needs a 5V/3A supply, like the 15W USB-C power supply.

Q: My Pi Zero won't turn on. Am I using the wrong power supply?
A: Pi Zero needs 5V/2.5A, use the official 12.5W Micro USB power supply.

Q: I've got a Pi 5 but only a 3A charger. Will that cause problems?
A: Yes, 5V/3A on a Pi 5 limits USB peripherals to only 600mA total.

Q: There are two USB ports that look the same on my Pi. Which one do I plug power into?
A: Use the port marked "POWER IN", "PWR IN", or "PWR" for power.

# Boot media

Q: Does the Raspberry Pi have built-in storage or do I need to bring my own?
A: Raspberry Pi models lack onboard storage, so you must supply your own.

Q: Can I boot from a USB stick instead of an SD card?
A: Yes, USB storage is supported, but only on more recent Raspberry Pi models.

Q: What size SD card do I need for Raspberry Pi OS?
A: At least 32 GB is recommended for Raspberry Pi OS installations.

Q: I'm installing the Lite version. Can I get away with a smaller SD card?
A: Yes, Raspberry Pi OS Lite only needs at least 16 GB of storage.

Q: I've got a 4TB SD card lying around. Will that work in a Pi?
A: No, cards above 2TB aren't supported due to MBR limitations.

Q: My Pi Zero won't boot from my big SD card. What's going on?
A: Pi Zero only boots from a boot partition of 256 GB or less due to hardware limits.

Q: Does a faster SD card actually make a difference?
A: Yes, faster read and write speeds improve performance on your Raspberry Pi.

# Display

Q: What kind of display port does the Raspberry Pi 5 have?
A: Raspberry Pi 5 has two micro HDMI ports for display output.

Q: My monitor doesn't have a micro HDMI port. Can I still connect it to my Pi 5?
A: Yes, use a micro-HDMI-to-HDMI cable to connect to any standard HDMI display.

Q: I've got two HDMI ports on my Pi. Which one should my main monitor go into?
A: Plug your primary monitor into the port marked HDMI0.

Q: Can I connect my monitor through the USB-C port on my Pi?
A: No, no Raspberry Pi models support video over USB-C DisplayPort alt mode.

Q: Does the Pi 4 have a headphone jack or audio out?
A: Yes, it has a 3.5mm TRRS jack for audio and composite output.

# Audio

Q: Can I get audio out of a Raspberry Pi without a headphone jack?
A: Yes, all Pi models with HDMI support audio output over HDMI.

Q: Does Raspberry Pi support Bluetooth speakers?
A: Yes, all Raspberry Pi models equipped with Bluetooth support Bluetooth audio.

Q: The audio from the headphone jack on my Pi seems really quiet. Is that normal?
A: Yes, the 3.5mm jack may require amplification for sufficient output volume.

Q: What ways can I get sound out of a Raspberry Pi?
A: You can use HDMI, USB, Bluetooth, or the 3.5mm jack depending on your model.

# Networking

Q: Does every Raspberry Pi have Wi-Fi built in?
A: No, only flagship models since Pi 3B, Keyboard models, Zero W, and Pico W models.

Q: My Pi doesn't have an Ethernet port. Can I still plug it into my router?
A: Yes, you can connect via wired Ethernet using a USB-to-Ethernet adapter.

Q: What does the "Model A" versus "Model B" naming actually mean?
A: Model B includes an Ethernet port; Model A does not have one.

# Whole Document

Q: Do I need a mouse and keyboard to set up a Raspberry Pi?
A: Only if using it directly with a desktop; headless setup needs neither.

Q: I'm totally new to this. What's the bare minimum I need to buy to get started?
A: At minimum you need a power supply and a microSD card with an OS.

Q: Can I use any old Bluetooth keyboard and mouse with a Raspberry Pi?
A: Yes, connect any Bluetooth keyboard or mouse using a USB Bluetooth receiver.
