# Whole Document

Q: What even is a keyboard computer? Is it just a keyboard or an actual computer?
A: It's a complete personal computer built into a keyboard form factor.

Q: How many keyboard computer models does Raspberry Pi make?
A: Raspberry Pi offers three keyboard computers: the 400, 500, and 500 plus.

Q: Do these keyboard computers use the same chips as the regular Pi boards?
A: Yes, they include the same system-on-chip as Raspberry Pi single-board computers.

Q: Do I need different accessories depending on where I live?
A: Yes, regional variants affect keyboard layout and required power supply.

# Features

Q: What's actually different between the Pi 400 and the newer 500 models? Is it worth upgrading?
A: The 500 models have faster CPU, more RAM, better GPU, and HDR support.

Q: How fast is the processor on the 500 compared to the 400?
A: The 400 runs at 1.8 GHz Cortex-A72; the 500 models run at 2.4 GHz Cortex-A76.

Q: I've got the 400 and I want to turn it off but there's no power button. What do I do?
A: Press Fn plus F10 to turn the Raspberry Pi 400 on or off.

Q: Does the 500 have a real power button or do I still need that key combo?
A: Both the 500 and 500 plus have a dedicated power key.

Q: Does the Pi 400 support HDR displays?
A: No, only the Raspberry Pi 500 and 500 plus support HDR on HDMI outputs.

Q: How much RAM does the 500 plus have compared to the regular 500?
A: The 500 has 8 GB of RAM; the 500 plus has 16 GB of RAM.

Q: What's special about the 500 plus that the regular 500 doesn't have?
A: The 500 plus has mechanical keys, RGB LEDs, replaceable keycaps, and built-in M.2 SSD.

Q: Does the 500 plus come with storage built in, or do I need a microSD card?
A: It includes an internal 256 GB M.2 SSD pre-installed with Raspberry Pi OS.

Q: How many USB ports do these keyboard computers have?
A: They each have two USB 3.0 ports and one USB 2.0 port.

Q: Does the Pi keyboard computer have GPIO pins like the regular Pi boards?
A: Yes, all models have a horizontal 40-pin GPIO header.

Q: How much power does the Pi 400 need compared to the 500?
A: The 400 needs 5V at 3A (15W); the 500 and 500 plus need 5V at 5A (25W).

Q: Do these things have Wi-Fi built in?
A: Yes, all models include dual-band Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Low Energy.

Q: What GPU does the 500 use and how does it compare to the 400's?
A: The 500 uses VideoCore VII at 910 MHz versus the 400's VideoCore VI at 500 MHz.

# Keyboard computer kits

Q: What do I get in the box if I buy a Pi 400 kit?
A: You get the keyboard, power supply, mouse, 16 GB microSD card, HDMI cable, and book.

Q: Is the microSD card in the 500 kit bigger than the one in the 400 kit?
A: Yes, the 500 kit includes a 32 GB A2-class card versus the 400 kit's 16 GB card.

Q: Does the 500 plus kit come with a microSD card too?
A: No, the 500 plus kit doesn't include a microSD card since it has a built-in SSD.

Q: What's that plastic tool included with the 500 plus kit for?
A: The spudger is for opening the keyboard to access the M.2 SSD inside.

Q: Why does the 500 plus kit include a keycap puller?
A: It's for removing and replacing the mechanical keycaps on the 500 plus keyboard.

# Getting started

Q: I just got my Pi 400 and I'm not sure where to start. Is there a guide?
A: Follow the Getting Started with Your Raspberry Pi instructions linked in the documentation.

Q: My 500 kit came with a microSD card with the OS already on it. Do I have to set everything up from scratch?
A: No, you can skip ahead to the Set Up Your Raspberry Pi section of the guide.

# Raspberry Pi 500+ mechanical keys

Q: What kind of switches does the 500 plus use under the keys?
A: It uses Gateron KS-33 low-profile mechanical switches with a custom grey clicky stem.

Q: I want to swap out the keycaps on my 500 plus. Are the stems a standard size?
A: Yes, they use Cherry MX-compatible cross-shaped stems, which is the industry standard.

Q: Why does the 500 plus have a grey stem instead of the usual coloured ones?
A: Raspberry Pi uses a custom grey stem instead of the standard colour-coded stems.

Q: What do the different stem colours on Gateron switches actually mean?
A: Coloured stems indicate switch type: clicky, tactile, or linear.

# Raspberry Pi 500+ customisation

Q: What can I actually customise on the 500 plus?
A: You can replace the SSD, swap keycaps, and adjust backlighting presets and brightness.

Q: I'm a more advanced user. Can I do more than just pick a lighting preset?
A: Yes, you can configure individual LEDs and remap keys on the fly.

# Replace your M.2 SSD

Q: Can I upgrade the SSD in my 500 plus to a bigger one?
A: Yes, you can replace the M.2 SSD to expand storage capacity.

Q: What M.2 form factors does the 500 plus support?
A: It supports M.2 M key devices in form factors 2230, 2242, 2260, and 2280.

Q: What tools do I need to swap the SSD in my 500 plus?
A: You need a cross head screwdriver, flat head screwdriver, and the included plastic spudger.

Q: I'm opening my 500 plus to replace the SSD. Do I just lift the top off?
A: No, fold the top over carefully; the halves stay connected by a flexible cable.

Q: How many screws do I need to remove to open the 500 plus?
A: There are five screws on the bottom of the keyboard to remove.

Q: Can I put something other than an SSD in that M.2 slot?
A: Yes, you can also use the slot to install a different PCIe peripheral.

# Change your keycaps

Q: Can I put any mechanical keycaps on my 500 plus or do they have to be a specific brand?
A: Most third-party keycaps designed for Cherry MX are compatible with the 500 plus.

Q: Are there any keycap profiles I should avoid for the 500 plus?
A: Avoid taller profiles like XDA, OEM, and SA as they can hit the frame or cause noise.

Q: How do I actually get a keycap off without breaking anything?
A: Use the keycap puller with equal pressure on both sides, then pull straight up gently.

Q: Can I take keycaps off just to clean underneath them?
A: Yes, you can remove keycaps to clean underneath them.

Q: I replaced a keycap but it feels wobbly. Did I put it on wrong?
A: Align the cross-shaped stem and press down firmly until the keycap feels fully seated.

# Keyboard layout

Q: I want to buy a replacement keycap set. What language layouts are easiest to find?
A: US layouts are most widely available, followed by UK layouts.

Q: Will any keycap set work perfectly with the 500 plus or are some keys non-standard?
A: Some keys like the power key, SysRq, and volume labels are non-standard and hard to match.

Q: My replacement keycap set doesn't have a Raspberry Pi logo key. What's that key normally called?
A: Third-party sets typically label that key as Win instead of Cmd or showing the Pi logo.

# Keycap profiles

Q: What keycap profile do you recommend for the 500 plus?
A: DSA or Cherry profiles are recommended for replacement keycaps on the 500 plus.

Q: What's the difference between DSA and Cherry profile keycaps?
A: DSA is flat and swappable across rows; Cherry is sculpted with different heights per row.

# Lighting

Q: I put on some really solid opaque keycaps and now I can't see the backlight. Why?
A: Fully opaque keycaps block illumination since LEDs light the top half of keycaps.

# Customise your backlighting

Q: I just turned on my 500 plus and most of the lights are off. Is something broken?
A: No, most backlighting is off by default; only the power LED and Caps Lock are enabled.

Q: What does the light behind the power button mean on the 500 plus?
A: Red means it's receiving power but off; green means it's on; blinking means SD card activity.

Q: There's a rainbow animation every time I start my 500 plus. Can I turn that off?
A: Advanced users can disable it per preset using the configuration software settings.

Q: How do I switch between the different lighting presets on my 500 plus?
A: Press Fn plus F4 to cycle forwards, or Fn plus Shift plus F4 to cycle backwards.

Q: How many lighting presets does the 500 plus come with?
A: It comes with seven presets, numbered zero through six.

Q: What's the Typing Heatmap preset? That sounds interesting.
A: Keys turn redder the more you press them, and surrounding keys light up too.

Q: I want my keyboard to light up when I press keys. Which preset does that?
A: Preset 6, called Solid Reactive Simple, lights keys up when you press them.

# Backlight controls

Q: How do I change the colour of my keyboard backlight?
A: Press Fn plus F3 to cycle forwards, or Fn plus Shift plus F3 to go backwards through 16 colours.

Q: My keyboard backlight is too bright. How do I dim it?
A: Press Fn plus F5 to reduce brightness, or Fn plus F6 to increase it.

Q: Does Shift always reverse the direction when I'm using the Fn key shortcuts?
A: Yes, Shift either cycles backwards through options or inverts the key function.

# Advanced software settings

Q: What can I do with that rpi-keyboard-config tool exactly?
A: You can configure LED settings, remap keys, and play a Flappy Bird game on the 500 plus.

Q: Does the advanced keyboard software work on the regular 500 or just the 500 plus?
A: Key remapping works on both; advanced LED settings are only for the 500 plus.

# Firmware setup

Q: How do I get the advanced keyboard software set up on my Pi?
A: Install rpi-keyboard-fw-update, run the update command, then install rpi-keyboard-config.

Q: Where can I find the source code for the keyboard firmware?
A: The firmware source code is in the Vial QMK repository on GitHub.

# Basic information commands

Q: How do I see what my keyboard looks like with all the key positions shown?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config info --ascii to see a keyboard layout diagram.

Q: I don't know what commands are available for the keyboard config tool. How do I find out?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config help to list all available commands with brief descriptions.

# Advanced LED settings

Q: Can I set each individual key to its own colour on the 500 plus?
A: Yes, you can control individual LEDs by configuring and applying the direct effect.

Q: Can I make different presets have different startup animations?
A: Yes, you can set a different startup animation for each individual preset.

# Lighting effects

Q: How do I find out what lighting effects are available for my 500 plus?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config list-effects to see all available lighting effects.

Q: If I apply a temporary lighting effect, does it overwrite my saved presets?
A: No, when you cycle presets again it continues from where you left off before.

Q: Can I change the speed of a lighting effect?
A: Yes, add --speed followed by a number between 0 and 255 to your effect command.

Q: What parameters can I adjust on lighting effects?
A: You can adjust speed, saturation, and hue, each as a number from 0 to 255.

Q: Does changing speed work on all effects or just some?
A: Some effects like solid colour are static and won't be affected by speed changes.

# View presets

Q: How do I check which lighting preset I'm currently using?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config preset index to show your current preset's index number.

Q: Can I jump straight to a specific preset without cycling through them all?
A: Yes, use rpi-keyboard-config preset index X where X is the preset number you want.

# Reassign presets

Q: If I mess up my presets, can I get the defaults back?
A: Yes, run rpi-keyboard-config reset-presets to restore all default presets.

Q: Will my custom presets survive a reboot?
A: Yes, preset changes are saved and persist every time you restart your 500 plus.

# Change preset startup animation

Q: How many different startup animation options are there?
A: There are five startup animation options including one for no animation at all.

Q: What's the default startup animation on the 500 plus?
A: The default is START_ANIM_B_FADE_VAL, a pulsing rainbow fading into the chosen effect.

Q: Can I completely turn off the startup animation for a preset?
A: Yes, use START_ANIM_NONE as the startup animation name to disable it entirely.

# Effect and preset parameters

Q: What's the default speed if I don't set one when applying an effect?
A: The default speed is 128 if you don't add the speed parameter.

Q: What's the default saturation for lighting effects?
A: The default saturation is 255, which is maximum saturation.

Q: I didn't set a hue for my effect. What colour will it use?
A: It uses the global hue set with Fn plus F3 on the keyboard by default.

# Global lighting controls

Q: How do I check what brightness my keyboard backlight is currently set to?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config brightness to show the current brightness level.

Q: What range of values can I use when setting the brightness?
A: Brightness is managed on a range of 0 to 255.

# Individual LED controls

Q: How do I turn off all the LEDs except the power button on my 500 plus?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config leds clear to turn off all LEDs except the power button.

Q: Can I set all keys to the same colour at once instead of one by one?
A: Yes, use rpi-keyboard-config leds set --colour followed by your chosen colour.

Q: How do I figure out the row and column coordinates for a specific key?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config info --ascii to see the keyboard layout diagram with coordinates.

Q: I set up individual LED colours I really like. How do I save them?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config leds save to save your current LED configuration.

Q: Can I use colour names like red or blue, or do I have to use numbers?
A: You can use common colour names, HSV numbers, or RGB numbers in quotation marks.

# Reset presets and LED customisations

Q: How do I reset everything back to the default lighting settings?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config reset-presets to restore defaults and clear LED customisations.

# Remap keys on your keyboard

Q: Can I remap keys on the regular 500 or just the 500 plus?
A: Key remapping is available on both the Raspberry Pi 500 and 500 plus.

Q: I set up my keys but I hate it. How do I get the default layout back?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config reset-keymap to reset all layers to default mappings.

# List keycodes

Q: How do I find the keycode for a key I want to remap to?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config list-keycodes to see all possible keycodes.

Q: There are loads of keycodes. Can I filter them down to just a category?
A: Yes, add --category followed by the category name to filter the keycode list.

# View keymap

Q: How do I see how all my keys are currently mapped?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config key get-all to see the full current key mapping.

Q: How do I check what a specific key is currently mapped to?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config key get with the row and column coordinates of that key.

# Remap keys

Q: How do I actually remap a key to do something different?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config key set followed by the row, column, and desired keycode.

# Watch key presses

Q: I want to see which key is which position on the keyboard. Can I watch key presses?
A: Yes, but you must first unlock the keyboard before you can watch key presses.

Q: How do I unlock my keyboard so I can watch key presses?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config unlock and hold the indicated keys until the countdown finishes.

Q: The lights flash red when I watch key presses and it's distracting. Can I turn that off?
A: Yes, add --no-leds to the key watch command to disable the light-up feedback.

Q: I'm done watching key presses. How do I lock my keyboard again?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config lock, which locks the keyboard immediately without confirmation.

Q: I've remapped my keys so I'm not sure which physical keys to hold for unlocking. How does it know?
A: The unlock sequence uses physical key positions, and the command tells you which to press.

# Manage layers

Q: How many layers can I set up for different key mappings?
A: You can edit four layers numbered 0 through 3; layer 0 is the default.

Q: What does the Fn key do with layers by default?
A: By default, the Fn key switches the keyboard from layer 0 to layer 1.

# Remap keys with Vial

Q: Can I remap my Pi 500 keys through a graphical interface instead of the terminal?
A: Yes, use the Vial web interface at vial.rocks to change key mappings graphically.

Q: Should I use Vial to customise the RGB lighting too?
A: No, Raspberry Pi recommends using Vial only for key mapping, not LED customisation.

# Return to default settings

Q: I messed up my keyboard layout so badly I can't type anything. Am I stuck?
A: Connect an external USB keyboard and run the reset command from that device instead.

Q: If I update the firmware, do I lose all my custom settings?
A: No, settings persist after firmware updates, but you can wipe them with a specific command.

Q: How do I completely wipe all my keyboard customisations and start fresh?
A: Run sudo rpi-keyboard-fw-update -w -i to clear all customisations and restore defaults.

# Flappy Bird game

Q: Wait, there's actually a game hidden in the keyboard software? How do I play it?
A: Run rpi-keyboard-config game to play a Flappy Bird-style game using the keyboard backlight.

Q: How do I control the Flappy Bird game on my 500 plus?
A: Press the space bar to fly over the pipe obstacles represented by lit-up LEDs.

Q: How do I know my score in the keyboard backlight game?
A: The number of purple LEDs along the top of the keyboard indicates your score.

Q: How do I exit the Flappy Bird game when I'm done?
A: Press Q on your keyboard to exit the game.
