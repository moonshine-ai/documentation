Q: What's the highest resolution video these Raspberry Pi cameras can record?
A: All supported cameras can record full HD 1080p video or better.

Q: I've got an old Pi project using raspistill — will that still work with newer cameras?
A: No, raspistill is deprecated, unsupported, and won't work with newer camera modules.

Q: I'm looking at cameras for my Pi and I don't know which one to pick. What's available?
A: Options include Camera Module 2, 3, High Quality, Global Shutter, and AI Camera.

Q: Does Raspberry Pi make an official camera? I wasn't sure if I needed a third-party one.
A: Yes, Raspberry Pi produces several official camera modules for their boards.

Q: What's special about the Global Shutter Camera compared to the others?
A: The Global Shutter Camera is specifically designed for fast motion photography.

Q: I'm still running Bullseye on my Pi. Can I use the newer camera modules with it?
A: The legacy camera stack on Bullseye won't ever support newer camera modules.

Q: What's the AI Camera and why would I want it over a regular camera module?
A: It uses Sony IMX500 to provide low-latency, high-performance AI capabilities to applications.

Q: I want to use a proper external lens with my Pi camera. Which camera supports that?
A: The High Quality Camera has CS and M12 mounts for use with external lenses.

Q: I've been using the old Picamera Python library. Is that still supported?
A: No, the original Picamera library is deprecated and unsupported; use Picamera2 instead.

Q: What do I need to do first before I can start using my camera module?
A: First install your camera module, then follow the guides in the section.

Q: What's the megapixel difference between Camera Module 2 and Camera Module 3?
A: Camera Module 2 is eight megapixels; Camera Module 3 is twelve megapixels.

Q: Can I get a Camera Module 3 without an infrared filter? Like for night vision stuff?
A: Yes, Camera Module 3 is available with or without an infrared filter.
