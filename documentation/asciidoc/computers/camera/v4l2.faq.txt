# V4L2 drivers

Q: What are V4L2 drivers even for? Like why do I need them?
A: They provide a standard Linux interface for accessing camera and codec features.

Q: Do I have to manually load the camera drivers or does that happen on its own?
A: Linux usually loads drivers automatically at boot, but sometimes you load them manually.

Q: I'm having trouble getting my camera to work. Could it be that the driver didn't load automatically?
A: Yes, in some situations you may need to load camera drivers explicitly.

# Device nodes when using `libcamera`

Q: What's the video0 device actually connected to?
A: It's the Unicam driver for the first CSI-2 receiver on your Pi.

Q: I've got two camera ports on my Pi. What device node handles the second one?
A: The second CSI-2 receiver uses the video1 Unicam driver.

Q: Which device node do I use if I want to do video encoding?
A: Video encoding is handled by the video11 device node.

Q: And what about decoding video, is there a separate node for that?
A: Yes, video decoding uses the video10 device node.

Q: What does video12 do exactly?
A: It's a simple ISP that handles RGB/YUV conversion, resizing, and Bayer to RGB/YUV conversion.

Q: What's the difference between video14 and video15?
A: Video14 gives high resolution ISP output; video15 gives low resolution ISP output.

Q: I want image statistics from the ISP. Which device node gives me that?
A: Image statistics from the fully programmable ISP come from video16.

Q: Is there a node specifically for HEVC decoding?
A: Yes, video19 handles HEVC decode.

Q: What's video13 used for?
A: It's the input node for the fully programmable ISP.
