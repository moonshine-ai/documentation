# `rpicam-vid`

Q: What's rpicam-vid actually for, like what does it do?
A: It captures video, shows a preview, and writes an encoded bitstream to output.

Q: I recorded a video with rpicam-vid and now I can't figure out how to watch it. What do I use?
A: You can play the resulting H.264 file using ffplay or other video players.

Q: I tried opening my H.264 file in VLC and it's just showing garbled frames. What's going on?
A: Recent VLC versions don't play H.264 files correctly; use a different player instead.

Q: How do I record a ten second video and save it to a file?
A: Run rpicam-vid with -t 10s and -o followed by your desired filename.

Q: Can I save directly as an MP4 instead of that raw H.264 thing?
A: On Pi 5 just use an mp4 file extension; on Pi 4 add the --codec libav flag.

Q: I'm on a Pi 4 and want to save as MP4. How do I do that?
A: On Pi 4 use rpicam-vid with -t, --codec libav, and -o pointing to an mp4 file.

Q: Does rpicam-vid use hardware encoding or is it doing everything in software?
A: It uses hardware H.264 encoding when available.

# Encoders

Q: Can I record in a format other than H.264? Like is there anything else supported?
A: rpicam-vid also supports motion JPEG and uncompressed unformatted YUV420 encoding.

Q: Does the file extension I use determine what format the video gets saved in?
A: No, the codec option determines output format, not the file extension.

Q: I want to save a bunch of individual JPEG photos from a video stream. Can I do that?
A: Yes, use mjpeg codec with --segment 1 and a counter in the output filename.

Q: What does the segment option actually do?
A: It breaks output files into chunks based on the segment size given in milliseconds.

# Capture high framerate video

Q: My high framerate video keeps dropping frames. What can I do to fix that?
A: Try setting H.264 level to 4.2, disabling denoise, hiding the preview, and lowering resolution.

Q: Should I turn off the preview window when recording fast video?
A: Yes, disabling the preview frees up extra CPU cycles helpful for high framerate recording.

Q: What resolution should I aim for if I'm trying to hit really high framerates?
A: Try 1280x720 or lower to achieve your target framerate.

Q: Can I overclock my Pi 4 to get better video performance?
A: Yes, add gpu_freq=550 or higher in /boot/firmware/config.txt to overclock the GPU.

Q: What's force_turbo and should I set it when recording video?
A: Setting force_turbo=1 in config.txt prevents the CPU clock from throttling during capture.

Q: How would I actually record 120fps video on a Pi?
A: Use --level 4.2, --framerate 120, --width 1280, --height 720, and --denoise cdn_off.

# `libav` integration with `rpicam-vid`

Q: Can rpicam-vid record audio too, not just video?
A: Yes, using the libav backend with --libav-audio enables audio recording alongside video.

Q: What even is libav and why would I use it instead of the default encoder?
A: It's the ffmpeg codec backend that lets you encode audio and video or stream over a network.

Q: How do I enable libav when recording video?
A: Pass libav to the codec option, like --codec libav, in your rpicam-vid command.

# Low latency video with the Pi 5

Q: I'm streaming live from a Pi 5 and there's too much delay. Is there a way to reduce it?
A: Add --low-latency to your rpicam-vid command to output encoded frames more quickly.

Q: What's the downside of using low latency mode on the Pi 5?
A: Coding efficiency drops slightly and CPU cores may be used less efficiently.

Q: Does low latency mode on Pi 5 mean I can't do 1080p anymore?
A: No, it still easily achieves 1080p30 even with the slight framerate reduction.

Q: Why does Pi 5 have more latency than older Pis when recording video?
A: Pi 5 uses software video encoders which output frames with longer latency than hardware encoders.
