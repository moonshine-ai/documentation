# Blink an LED

Q: How do I make the LED on my Pico blink?
A: Download the Blink UF2, hold BOOTSEL, plug in, then drag and drop.

Q: Where's the LED actually connected on the Pico?
A: The onboard LED is connected to GP25 on the RP2040 chip.

Q: I've got a Pico W, is the LED in a different place than the regular Pico?
A: Yes, Pico W's LED connects to WL_GPIO0 on the Infineon 43439 wireless chip.

Q: My Pico isn't showing up when I plug it in. What button do I need to hold?
A: Hold the BOOTSEL button while plugging into the USB port.

Q: What's the drive called when my Pico mounts as storage?
A: It mounts as a Mass Storage Device called RPI-RP2.

Q: Where can I find the actual code behind the blink example?
A: The blink code for both Pico and Pico W is available on GitHub.

# Say "Hello World"

Q: I want to see Hello World on my Pico. What do I need to do?
A: Download the UF2, drag it onto RPI-RP2, then use minicom to read output.

Q: What baud rate do I use when I'm connecting with minicom to my Pico?
A: Use 115200 as the baud rate when connecting with minicom.

Q: I don't have minicom installed. How do I get it?
A: Run sudo apt install minicom to install it on your computer.

Q: What device name do I use to connect to the Pico with minicom?
A: Use /dev/ttyACM0 as the device when running minicom.

# Whole Document

Q: I just got a Pico. What are the first two things I should try?
A: Blink the onboard LED, then print Hello World over USB serial.

Q: Do I need to install any special software to copy programs onto my Pico?
A: No, just drag and drop UF2 files onto the RPI-RP2 mass storage volume.

Q: Is the setup process different for Pico versus Pico W?
A: The process is the same, but you download a different Blink UF2 file.
