# Whole Document

Q: What languages can I use to program a Raspberry Pi Pico?
A: You can program Raspberry Pi Pico using MicroPython, C, or C++.

Q: Does the Raspberry Pi Pico run Linux?
A: No, Raspberry Pi Pico doesn't run Linux or support removable storage.

Q: How many generations of the Pico are there, and what are they called?
A: There are two generations: the Raspberry Pi Pico and Raspberry Pi Pico 2.

Q: What's the difference between the W and non-W versions of the Pico?
A: W variants include Wi-Fi and Bluetooth; non-W variants have no wireless connectivity.

Q: I'm trying to pick between a Pico and a Pico H — what's actually different between them?
A: The Pico H has presoldered header pins; otherwise hardware capabilities are identical.

Q: How do you actually put a program onto a Pico?
A: You flash binaries to the on-board flash memory to program a Raspberry Pi Pico.

# Raspberry Pi Pico

Q: What chip is inside the original Raspberry Pi Pico?
A: The first-generation Raspberry Pi Pico is based on the RP2040 microcontroller chip.

Q: How many GPIO pins does the Pico have?
A: The Raspberry Pi Pico has 26 multi-function GPIO pins.

Q: How much memory does the Pico have built in?
A: The Raspberry Pi Pico has 264 kB of SRAM and 2 MB of on-board flash memory.

Q: Can I just drag and drop files to program the Pico, or do I need special software?
A: Yes, drag-and-drop programming works using mass storage over USB.

Q: How fast can the Pico's processor run?
A: The RP2040's flexible clock runs up to 133 MHz.

Q: Does the Pico have Bluetooth built in?
A: Only the Pico W and Pico WH variants include Bluetooth; standard Pico does not.

Q: What wireless standard does the Pico W use for Wi-Fi?
A: Pico W uses Wi-Fi 802.11n on a single 2.4 GHz band.

Q: How many devices can connect to the Pico W if I use it as a hotspot?
A: The Pico W soft access point supports up to four clients.

Q: I want to use my Pico W commercially — do I need to pay for the wireless libraries?
A: No, Raspberry Pi grants a free commercial license for projects using Pico W boards.

Q: Can I freely use and modify the Pico design files for my own products?
A: Yes, design files are openly available and can be used, copied, and modified freely.

# Non-wireless Raspberry Pi Pico boards

Q: Are the Pico and Pico H otherwise identical other than the headers?
A: Yes, both have identical hardware capabilities; only headers and debug connector style differ.

Q: Is the pinout on the Pico the same as on the Pico 2?
A: Yes, the pinout and layout are the same for Pico, Pico H, Pico 2, and Pico 2 with headers.

Q: How many PWM channels does the Pico have?
A: The Raspberry Pi Pico has 16 PWM channels.

Q: Does the Pico have a built-in temperature sensor?
A: Yes, the RP2040 chip includes a temperature sensor.

# Wireless Raspberry Pi Pico boards

Q: What version of Bluetooth does the Pico W support?
A: The Pico W supports Bluetooth 5.2, including BLE and Bluetooth Classic.

Q: Does the Pico W support WPA3 security?
A: Yes, the Pico W supports WPA3 for wireless connectivity.

Q: Is the pinout the same on the Pico W as on the Pico 2 W?
A: Yes, Pico W and Pico WH share the same pinout as Pico 2 W and Pico 2 W with headers.

# Raspberry Pi Pico 2

Q: What chip is in the Pico 2?
A: The Raspberry Pi Pico 2 is based on the RP2350 microcontroller chip.

Q: How much flash storage does the Pico 2 have compared to the original Pico?
A: Pico 2 has 4 MB of on-board flash, double the Pico's 2 MB.

Q: How fast can the Pico 2 run?
A: The RP2350's flexible clock runs up to 150 MHz.

Q: I've got existing Pico 1 code — will it work on the Pico 2?
A: Yes, Pico 2 has hardware and software compatibility with Raspberry Pi Pico 1.

Q: What CPU options does the Pico 2 have? I heard it can use RISC-V?
A: Pico 2 offers dual-core Arm Cortex-M33 or dual-core Hazard3 RISC-V processor.

Q: How is the naming different between Pico 1 headers variants and Pico 2?
A: Pico 2 says "with headers" in full; Pico 1 shortens it to "H" in the name.

Q: How many PWM channels does the Pico 2 have?
A: The Raspberry Pi Pico 2 has 24 PWM channels.

Q: How much RAM does the Pico 2 have?
A: The Pico 2 has 520 kB of SRAM.

# Wireless models

Q: What wireless chip do the Pico W and Pico 2 W use?
A: Wireless Pico boards use the Infineon CYW43439 wireless chip.

Q: I want to put the Pico W in an enclosure — is there anything I need to watch out for with the antenna?
A: Keep antenna space free; nearby metal can significantly reduce signal gain and bandwidth.

Q: Why can't I read the voltage while doing wireless stuff on the Pico W?
A: The SPI clock shares the VSYS monitor pin, so ADC can't read VSYS during SPI transactions.

# Board layouts

Q: Where's the BOOTSEL button on the Pico?
A: BOOTSEL is located under the USB port and to the left side of the board.

Q: Where's the USB port on the Pico — is it at the top or the side?
A: The Micro USB port for power and data is located at the top edge of the board.

Q: How many pins does the Pico have in total?
A: There are 40 pins total, 20 on each side of the board.

Q: I want to turn off my Pico from software — is there a pin for that?
A: Pulling the 3V3_EN pin at pin 37 to ground turns off the Raspberry Pi Pico.

Q: What's the VBUS pin actually connected to?
A: VBUS at pin 40 provides 5 V input directly from USB.

# Debug connector (SWD)

Q: What signals are on the SWD debug connector?
A: The three-pin connector carries SWDIO, GND, and SWCLK.

Q: Can I power my Pico through the debug connector?
A: No, there's no power pin; the board must be powered separately over USB or VSYS.

Q: Where's the debug connector on the Pico W versus the regular Pico?
A: On wireless boards it's below the microcontroller centrally; non-wireless boards have it at the bottom edge.

Q: My Pico H has a different debug connector than my plain Pico — what type is it?
A: Boards with headers use a small keyed three-pin JST-style connector.

# Software utilities

Q: How do I find out what's programmed on my Pico right now?
A: Use the picotool command-line tool to extract application name and version metadata.

Q: Can I use one Pico to debug another Pico?
A: Yes, install debugprobe firmware to use one Pico as a USB-to-SWD and UART converter.

Q: I think I've totally messed up my Pico — can I brick it permanently?
A: No, BOOTSEL mode is in read-only memory and can't be overwritten, preventing permanent bricking.

Q: How do I completely wipe the flash on my Pico and start fresh?
A: Copy a special UF2 flash-erase file to the Pico while it's in BOOTSEL mode.

Q: How do I put the Pico into BOOTSEL mode?
A: Hold the BOOTSEL button while connecting the board to your computer.
