# Enable Two-factor authentication

Q: What even is two-factor authentication and why would I need it?
A: 2FA adds extra login protection beyond just your password using a second factor.

Q: Does Raspberry Pi ID support two-factor authentication?
A: Yes, Raspberry Pi ID supports 2FA using a time-based one-time password.

Q: I've got an iPhone — can I use iCloud Keychain for this instead of downloading an app?
A: Yes, right-click the QR code for a "Set up verification code" option on iPhone.

# Install a 2FA application

Q: What apps can I use for two-factor authentication with my Raspberry Pi account?
A: You can use any 2FA app like Authy or Google Authenticator on your phone.

Q: Do I need a specific app or will any authenticator work?
A: Any 2FA app works; the guide suggests Authy or Google Authenticator as examples.

# Enable 2FA

Q: Okay I've got the app installed — how do I actually turn on two-factor authentication?
A: Click the Two-factor authentication option when signed into your Raspberry Pi ID.

Q: How do I connect my phone's authenticator app to my Raspberry Pi account?
A: Scan the QR code shown in Raspberry Pi ID with your 2FA app to start.

Q: What do I do after scanning the QR code?
A: Enter the six-digit TOTP from your 2FA app to register it with your account.

Q: I lost my phone and now I can't log in — is there any way around the two-factor thing?
A: The recovery code shown after setup is the only way to bypass 2FA.

Q: Where do I find the recovery code and how important is it really?
A: It appears on a confirmation screen after setup; store it safely as a backup.

Q: Can I turn off two-factor authentication later if I change my mind?
A: Yes, you can disable 2FA at any time at id.raspberrypi.com.

Q: Once I've set this up, what happens every time I log in?
A: You'll need to enter a TOTP generated by your 2FA app on your phone each time.
