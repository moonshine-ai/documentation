# Raspberry Pi Monitor (General)

Q: What size is the Raspberry Pi Monitor and how does it connect?
A: It's a 15.6-inch 1080p display that connects via HDMI cable.

Q: Does the Raspberry Pi Monitor work with all Raspberry Pi models?
A: Yes, it's compatible with all Raspberry Pi models that support HDMI output.

Q: What do I need to power the Raspberry Pi Monitor?
A: It needs a USB-C power source, ideally USB-PD capable of at least 1.5A.

# Controls

Q: Where are the buttons on this monitor? I can't find them.
A: The buttons are on the back of the monitor.

Q: What can I actually control with the buttons on the back?
A: You can control standby mode, display brightness, and speaker volume.

Q: How do I put the monitor into standby?
A: Press the button marked with the power symbol to enter or exit standby.

# On Screen Display Messages

Q: There's some weird message on my screen, what does it mean when it says no HDMI signal?
A: It means the monitor isn't detecting an HDMI signal from your connected device.

Q: My monitor just showed a message and went dark. What happened?
A: It entered standby mode to conserve power after warning you with an OSD message.

Q: I'm getting an unsupported resolution message. What does that mean?
A: Your connected device's display resolution isn't supported by the monitor.

Q: The brightness seems really dim and I can't turn it up much. Is something wrong?
A: You're likely in Power Saving mode because your power supply can't deliver 1.5A.

Q: I pressed the standby button but the screen stayed off and there was a little red light. What's that about?
A: The red LED means no HDMI signal was detected, so standby mode couldn't exit.

Q: How does the volume scale work on that little on-screen display thing?
A: Both brightness and volume show on a scale from 0 to 100.

# Position the Monitor

Q: Can I mount this monitor on a wall or arm instead of using the stand?
A: Yes, use the four VESA mount holes on the back of the red rear plastic housing.

Q: I want to hang the monitor on the wall without a VESA mount. Is there another way?
A: Flip the stand up, drill two holes 55mm apart, and hang it using the stand slots.

Q: Do I need spacers if I'm using a VESA mount?
A: Yes, use spacers to ensure adequate space for display and power cable egress.

# Power the Monitor

Q: Can I just use any USB-C charger to power the monitor?
A: It needs USB Power Delivery support; many USB-C supplies including official Pi ones work.

Q: What's the difference between Full Power mode and Power Saving mode?
A: Full Power allows 0-100% brightness and volume; Power Saving limits them to 50% and 60%.

Q: I'm powering the monitor directly from my Raspberry Pi's USB port. Will that work?
A: It'll work but only in Power Saving mode, since Pi devices can't provide 1.5A over USB-A.

Q: My monitor keeps flashing on and off. What's going on?
A: Your USB power supply can't provide enough current to reliably power the monitor.

Q: How do I switch from Power Saving mode to Full Power mode?
A: Press and hold the increase brightness button for 3 seconds.

Q: I've got a Pi 5 with a 3A power supply and the monitor keeps flickering. How do I fix it?
A: Reduce brightness and volume, or switch to a different power source or cable.

Q: Will the official Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 power supply work with this monitor?
A: Yes, the official Raspberry Pi 4 and 5 power supplies support USB-PD.

# Specification

Q: What resolutions does the Raspberry Pi Monitor actually support?
A: It supports 1920×1080, 1280×720, 720×576, 720×480, and 640×480 at 50 or 60Hz.

Q: Does this monitor have built-in speakers?
A: Yes, it has two 1.2-watt stereo speakers.

Q: Is there a headphone jack on this monitor?
A: Yes, there's a 3.5mm audio jack.

Q: How much does the Raspberry Pi Monitor weigh?
A: It weighs 850 grams.

Q: What's the contrast ratio on this monitor?
A: The contrast ratio is 800:1.

Q: How much power does this monitor actually use?
A: It uses 4.5 to 6.5 watts during use and under 0.1 watts at idle.
