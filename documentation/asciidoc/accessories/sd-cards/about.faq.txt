# About

Q: Does the SD card actually matter for a Raspberry Pi or can I just use any old one?
A: SD card quality critically affects overall user experience on a Raspberry Pi.

Q: What's so special about the official Raspberry Pi SD cards compared to random ones?
A: They support DDR50, SDR104 bus speeds and command queueing for optimal performance.

Q: What's command queueing and why do I care about it for my Pi?
A: It allows pipelining of random read operations, ensuring optimal SD card performance.

Q: Can I get a Pi SD card that already has the operating system on it?
A: Yes, Raspberry Pi SD cards are available pre-programmed with the latest Raspberry Pi OS.

Q: What sizes do the official Raspberry Pi SD cards come in?
A: They're available in 32 GB, 64 GB, and 128 GB sizes.

# Specifications

Q: What speed class rating do the official Pi SD cards have? Like U1 or U3?
A: Raspberry Pi SD cards are rated C10, U3, V30, and A2.

Q: What form factor are the Raspberry Pi SD cards, like full size or micro?
A: They use the microSDHC and microSDXC form factor.

Q: How fast is the official SD card on a Pi 5 compared to a Pi 4?
A: Pi 5 gets 5,000 read and 2,000 write IOPS versus Pi 4's 3,200 and 1,200.

Q: What spec standard do the Raspberry Pi SD cards follow?
A: Raspberry Pi SD cards use the SD6.1 SD specification.

Q: I've got a Pi 4 — what read and write speeds can I actually expect from the official card?
A: Pi 4 gets 3,200 IOPS read and 1,200 IOPS write using 4 kB random data.
