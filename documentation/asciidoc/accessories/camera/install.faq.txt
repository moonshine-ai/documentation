Q: I just got a Raspberry Pi camera. What do I actually need to set it up?
A: You need a Pi board with camera connector, a camera, and the right cable.

Q: Do I need to worry about static electricity when handling the camera?
A: Yes, earth yourself before touching the camera PCB to avoid static damage.

Q: Is the camera installation process different depending on which Pi I have?
A: The process is broadly the same, but connectors differ between boards.

# Step 1. Prepare

Q: There's a blue plastic film on my camera lens. Should I take it off?
A: Yes, it's just protective shipping film — gently peel it off before use.

Q: What cable do I need for a Raspberry Pi 5 camera?
A: Raspberry Pi 5 uses a mini 22-pin connector, so use a Standard-Mini cable.

Q: I've got a Pi 4. What camera cable do I need?
A: Pi 4 uses the standard 15-pin connector, so use the Standard-Standard cable.

Q: What about Pi Zero — what cable does that take?
A: Pi Zero uses the mini 22-pin connector, so get a Standard-Mini camera cable.

Q: I don't have an earthing strap. How do I ground myself before touching the camera?
A: Just touch a sink tap or something similar to earth yourself before handling it.

# Step 2. Connect the cable to your Raspberry Pi

Q: Where exactly is the camera connector on a Pi 4?
A: It's on the edge closest to you, between the micro HDMI connector and audio jack.

Q: I have a Pi 5 and can't figure out where the camera connector is.
A: On Pi 5, camera connectors are between the micro HDMI and Ethernet port, labelled CAM/DISP0 and CAM/DISP1.

Q: I've got a Pi 5 with an Active Cooler. Does that affect connecting the camera?
A: Connect the camera cable before installing the Active Cooler, as it blocks access afterwards.

Q: Do I need to turn my Pi off before plugging in the camera cable?
A: Yes, shut down and disconnect from power before connecting the camera cable.

Q: Which way do the metal contacts face when I insert the cable?
A: Insert the cable with metallic contacts facing away from the flap.

Q: On Pi 5 there are two camera connectors. Does it matter which one I use?
A: No, you can use either CAM/DISP0 or CAM/DISP1 for your camera.

Q: Where's the camera connector on an older Pi, like a Pi 3?
A: It's near the edge closest to you, between the HDMI connector and audio jack, labelled CAMERA.

Q: Where is the camera port on a Pi Zero? I can't find it.
A: It's on the short right edge of the board, opposite the SD card slot.

# Step 3. Connect the cable to the camera

Q: Which side of the camera board is the connector on?
A: The connector is on the opposite side of the board from the camera lens.

Q: My camera already came with a cable attached. Do I need to redo the connection?
A: No, cameras come with the Standard-Standard cable already attached — just use it.

Q: How do I know the cable is properly connected to the camera?
A: Push the flap down until it clicks, ensuring the cable is straight and contacts are aligned.

# Step 4. Prepare the software

Q: I've plugged the camera in. Now what do I do to get it working?
A: Update your system, then follow the setup instructions for rpicam-apps to get started.

Q: Can I use Python with the Raspberry Pi camera?
A: Yes, optionally install the Picamera2 Python library following its setup instructions.
