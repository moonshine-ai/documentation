# Whole Document

Q: What's an AI HAT and why would I want one for my Pi?
A: It adds a Hailo NPU chip for running AI models locally on Raspberry Pi 5.

Q: Do I need internet to run AI models with one of these HATs?
A: No, the Hailo NPU processes AI locally, removing the need for cloud servers.

Q: I've got a Raspberry Pi 5. Will these AI HATs work with it?
A: Yes, AI HAT+ and AI HAT+ 2 are designed specifically for Raspberry Pi 5.

Q: What's the difference between AI HAT+ and AI HAT+ 2?
A: AI HAT+ 2 has more TOPS, onboard memory, and supports LLMs and VLMs.

# How it works

Q: Does my Pi automatically know an AI HAT is connected, or do I have to set something up?
A: Raspberry Pi OS automatically detects the accelerator when an AI HAT is connected.

Q: I've plugged in the AI HAT. Does it just work, or do I need to install stuff?
A: You must also install software components and AI models for hardware-accelerated inference.

Q: Can I use the camera with the AI HAT for things like recognizing objects?
A: Yes, rpicam-apps and Picamera2 can use the Hailo NPU for image recognition and object detection.

Q: What kind of AI tasks can the camera frameworks do with the HAT?
A: They support AI computing tasks like image recognition and object detection automatically.

# Models

Q: I heard there's something called an AI Kit. Is that the same as the AI HAT+?
A: The AI Kit matches the Hailo-8L variant of AI HAT+ but is no longer in production.

Q: Can I run a chatbot locally on the AI HAT+ 2?
A: Yes, AI HAT+ 2 supports local LLMs and VLMs, enabling applications like local chat.

Q: Does the regular AI HAT+ support large language models?
A: No, LLM and VLM support is not available on AI HAT+, only on AI HAT+ 2.

Q: How much memory does the AI HAT+ 2 have?
A: It has 8 GB of onboard memory, supporting LLMs and VLMs up to around six billion parameters.

Q: What's the AI HAT+ good for if it can't do LLMs?
A: It suits object detection, camera post-processing, robotics, and moderate neural workloads.

# Performance (TOPS) variants

Q: What does TOPS mean and why does it matter?
A: TOPS means tera-operations per second, measuring the AI inference performance of the chip.

Q: What's the difference between the 13 TOPS and 26 TOPS versions of the AI HAT+?
A: The 26 TOPS version supports larger networks, higher throughput, and parallel AI models.

Q: Which chip is inside the 40 TOPS AI HAT+ 2?
A: It's built around the Hailo-10H neural network inference accelerator.

Q: I want to run bigger AI models with more throughput. Which AI HAT should I get?
A: The 26 TOPS AI HAT+ or the 40 TOPS AI HAT+ 2 support larger and more intensive workloads.

# Physical specifications

Q: Are the AI HAT+ and AI HAT+ 2 the same size?
A: Yes, both are approximately 66 mm by 56.5 mm; the main visual difference is the SDRAM.

Q: Does the AI HAT+ 2 come with a heatsink?
A: Yes, AI HAT+ 2 includes a heatsink approximately 42.5 mm by 64 mm.

# Hardware assembly

Q: What tools do I need to install an AI HAT onto my Pi 5?
A: You need a Phillips crosshead screwdriver; an Active Cooler is also recommended.

Q: What hardware comes in the box with an AI HAT?
A: It includes spacers, long and short screws, a GPIO stacking header, and a ribbon cable.

Q: Should I turn off my Pi before putting the AI HAT on?
A: Yes, ensure your Raspberry Pi 5 is disconnected from power before you begin installation.

Q: Do I need to buy a cooler separately for my Pi when using the AI HAT?
A: A Raspberry Pi Active Cooler is recommended but sold separately; AI HAT+ 2 includes its own heatsink.

Q: How does the AI HAT actually connect to the Pi 5?
A: It connects via the Raspberry Pi 5's PCIe port using a ribbon cable.

Q: Can I take the heatsink off the AI HAT+ 2 after I've put it on?
A: Removing the heatsink can degrade push pins and thermal pads, potentially causing product damage.

Q: Which way does the ribbon cable go in? I don't want to put it in backwards.
A: Insert it with the metallic contact points facing inwards, towards the USB ports.

Q: Do I need a heatsink on the AI HAT+ 2 or is it optional?
A: It's optional but recommended, especially when running benchmarks or intensive AI workloads.
