# About

Q: What does the TV HAT actually let me do with my Raspberry Pi?
A: It lets you receive, view, or stream digital terrestrial TV over your network.

Q: I've got an older Pi with 40 pins, will the TV HAT work on it?
A: Yes, the TV HAT works with any 40-pin Raspberry Pi board.

Q: I want to use my Pi just as a TV server for other devices. Which Pi do I need?
A: Any 40-pin Raspberry Pi works as a server for other devices on the network.

Q: What Pi do you recommend if I want to actually watch TV on the Pi itself?
A: Raspberry Pi 2 or later is recommended for viewing TV directly on the Pi.

Q: What tuner chip is inside the TV HAT?
A: It uses a Sony CXD2880 TV tuner chip.

# About DVB-T

Q: I'm in the US — will this TV HAT work for me?
A: No, the TV HAT doesn't support ATSC, the digital TV standard used in North America.

Q: What's DVB-T anyway? Like, what does it stand for?
A: It stands for Digital Video Broadcasting Terrestrial, a European digital TV broadcast standard.

Q: Does this thing work in other countries besides Europe?
A: It works where DVB-T or DVB-T2 standards are used, but not in North America.

# Install

Q: How do I actually physically attach the TV HAT to my Raspberry Pi?
A: Press the HAT over the GPIO pins, add corner spacers, tighten screws, then connect your aerial.

Q: What software do I need to install to actually watch TV through the HAT?
A: Install TVHeadend using sudo apt update then sudo apt install tvheadend.

Q: After I install TVHeadend, how do I open it up and get to it?
A: On another computer, open a browser and go to http://raspberrypi.local:9981/extjs.html.

Q: It's asking me for a username and password when I open TVHeadend in the browser. Where do I get those?
A: Use the administrator account name and password you chose during TVHeadend installation.

Q: I don't care about restricting access. What should I put in the network and login fields?
A: Leave Allowed Network blank and enter an asterisk in both username and password fields.

Q: How do I find out which TV transmitter to select for my area?
A: Use the Freeview website and enter your postcode to find your local transmitter.

Q: The video in my browser is really choppy. Is that normal and what should I do?
A: Yes, it can happen; they recommend using a local media player like VLC instead.

Q: How do I watch a channel in VLC rather than the browser?
A: Click the i icon next to a channel to find a stream file you can download.

Q: Are there any apps that work with TVHeadend for watching on my phone?
A: Yes, TVHeadend supports apps like TvhClient for iOS.

Q: The scan finished. How do I know if it actually found any channels?
A: After scanning you should see found muxes and found services counts displayed.

Q: What boxes should I tick in the Service Mapping window during setup?
A: Tick Map all services, Create provider tags, and Create network tags.

# Whole Document

Q: Can this HAT only tune into one channel at a time or multiple?
A: It can decode one mux at a time, but each mux can contain several channels.

Q: I just want to stream TV around my house. Do I need a powerful Pi for that?
A: Any 40-pin Raspberry Pi works as a TV server for streaming to other devices.
