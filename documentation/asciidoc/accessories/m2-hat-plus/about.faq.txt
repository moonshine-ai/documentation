# About

Q: What's the difference between the M.2 HAT+ and the M.2 HAT+ Compact?
A: They differ in size, case compatibility, Active Cooler support, and supported drive form factors.

Q: Can I plug an NVMe drive into my Raspberry Pi 5 with one of these HATs?
A: Yes, both M.2 HAT+ boards connect NVMe drives to Raspberry Pi 5's PCIe interface.

Q: Will the M.2 HAT+ fit inside the official Raspberry Pi 5 case?
A: Only if you remove the lid and the included fan from the case.

Q: I've got the compact version — does it work with the official Pi 5 case?
A: Yes, the M.2 HAT+ Compact is designed to fit around the case's included fan.

Q: Can I use the Active Cooler with the compact version?
A: No, you can't fit the Active Cooler beneath the M.2 HAT+ Compact.

Q: Does Raspberry Pi OS automatically recognize these HATs or do I have to set something up?
A: Both HATs conform to HAT+ spec, so Raspberry Pi OS automatically detects them.

# Features

Q: How fast is the connection on these M.2 HATs?
A: It's a single-lane PCIe 2.0 interface with a 500 MB/s peak transfer rate.

Q: How much power can the M.2 HAT+ supply to my drive?
A: It can supply up to 3 A to connected M.2 devices.

Q: What drive sizes does the regular M.2 HAT+ support?
A: It supports devices using the 2230 or 2242 form factor.

Q: I only have a 2242 drive — will the compact version work?
A: No, the M.2 HAT+ Compact only supports the 2230 form factor.

Q: Does the M.2 HAT+ have any lights on it so I know it's working?
A: Yes, both HATs have power and activity LEDs.

# Hardware

Q: What comes in the box with the M.2 HAT+?
A: The box includes a ribbon cable, threaded spacers, screws, and one knurled drive attachment screw.

Q: Does the compact version come with a GPIO stacking header?
A: No, only the standard M.2 HAT+ includes the 16 mm GPIO stacking header.

Q: Do I need anything else besides the HAT to get this working?
A: Yes, you also need a Raspberry Pi 5.

# Prepare your Raspberry Pi

Q: Do I need to update anything on my Pi before installing the M.2 HAT+?
A: Yes, update your software and ensure your firmware is dated December 6, 2023 or later.

Q: How do I check if my Pi's firmware is new enough for the M.2 HAT+?
A: Run `sudo rpi-eeprom-update` and check if the date is December 6, 2023 or later.

Q: My firmware is outdated. How do I update it before installing the HAT?
A: Run `sudo raspi-config`, choose Latest under Advanced Options, then run `sudo rpi-eeprom-update -a`.

Q: Should I leave my Pi plugged in while I'm installing the HAT?
A: No, disconnect your Raspberry Pi from power before beginning installation.

# Install the M.2 HAT+

Q: Should I install the Active Cooler before or after putting on the M.2 HAT+?
A: Install the Active Cooler first, before installing the M.2 HAT+.

Q: Does it matter which way around I push the GPIO stacking header on?
A: Orientation doesn't matter as long as all pins fit into place.

Q: Which way does the ribbon cable go into the PCIe port on the Pi?
A: Insert it with the copper contact points facing inward, towards the USB ports.

Q: How do I insert my SSD into the M.2 slot without breaking anything?
A: Slide the drive in at a slight upward angle gently — don't force it into the slot.

Q: How tight should I make the drive attachment screw?
A: Tighten until the SSD feels secure, but don't over-tighten the screw.

Q: Which way does the ribbon cable go into the M.2 HAT+ itself?
A: Insert it with the copper contact points facing up into the HAT+ slot.

# Install the M.2 HAT+ Compact

Q: Is installing the compact version basically the same as the regular one?
A: It's similar but the compact version doesn't use a GPIO stacking header during installation.

Q: For the compact version, which way do I insert the ribbon cable into the Pi's PCIe port?
A: Insert it with the copper contact points facing inward, towards the USB ports.

# Start your Raspberry Pi

Q: Once I've got the HAT installed, how do I find my NVMe drive on the Pi?
A: Without a desktop, find the drive at `/dev/nvme0n1`.

Q: Is it safe to just unplug my NVMe drive while the Pi is running?
A: No, always disconnect your Pi from power before connecting or disconnecting anything from the M.2 slot.

Q: I plugged everything in and I'm on the desktop — how do I know it's working?
A: You'll see an icon representing the drive appear on your desktop.

# Boot from NVMe

Q: How do I get my Pi to boot from the NVMe drive instead of the SD card?
A: In `raspi-config`, go to Advanced Options, Boot Order, and choose NVMe/USB boot.

Q: If I take out the SD card, will it just automatically boot from the NVMe?
A: Yes, without an SD card inserted, Raspberry Pi 5 boots automatically from the NVMe drive.

Q: How do I actually put an OS onto the NVMe drive?
A: Use Raspberry Pi Imager to install an operating system directly to your NVMe drive.

# Enable PCIe Gen 3

Q: Can I enable PCIe Gen 3 on my Pi 5 for faster speeds with this HAT?
A: You can enable it, but Raspberry Pi 5 isn't certified for Gen 3 and connections may be unstable.

# Whole Document

A: Only Raspberry Pi 5 is compatible with the M.2 HAT+ and M.2 HAT+ Compact.

A: You need either the M.2 HAT+ or M.2 HAT+ Compact plus a compatible M.2 M key drive.
