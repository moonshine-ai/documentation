# Specifications

Q: What sizes does the Touch Display 2 come in?
A: The Touch Display 2 comes in five-inch and seven-inch sizes.

Q: Do the five-inch and seven-inch versions work differently or are they basically the same?
A: Both sizes have identical features and functionality, just different physical sizes.

Q: What's the resolution on this thing?
A: The Touch Display 2 has a resolution of 720 by 1280 pixels.

Q: How many fingers can it detect at once?
A: It supports up to five simultaneous touch points at once.

Q: Do I need a separate power supply for the display?
A: No, it's powered directly by the host Raspberry Pi, no separate supply needed.

Q: Can I use it without a mouse or keyboard plugged in?
A: Yes, it supports full desktop control including selecting, dragging, scrolling, and menus.

# Hardware

Q: What cables come in the box with the display?
A: The box includes a 15-way FFC, a 22-way to 15-way FFC, and a GPIO power cable.

Q: I've got a Raspberry Pi 5 — which cable do I use to connect the display?
A: For Raspberry Pi 5, use the included 22-way to 15-way FFC cable.

Q: Will this display work with my Raspberry Pi Zero?
A: No, the Zero series lacks a DSI connector and isn't compatible with Touch Display 2.

Q: Which Raspberry Pi models does this display actually work with?
A: It works with all models from Raspberry Pi 1B onwards, except Zero and Keyboard series.

# Connectors

Q: How does the display actually connect to the Pi?
A: It uses a DSI connector for video and touch data, plus the GPIO header for power.

# Connect to Raspberry Pi

Q: How long does it take before I see something on the screen after plugging it in?
A: It can take up to one minute for Raspberry Pi OS to start displaying output.

Q: Do I need to turn my Pi off before connecting the display?
A: Yes, disconnect your Raspberry Pi from power before connecting the display.

Q: Which end of the cable goes into the display itself?
A: Insert the 15-way end into the Touch Display 2 FFC connector, metal contacts facing upwards.

Q: Which way do the metal contacts face when I plug the cable into the Pi?
A: Metal contacts should face toward the Ethernet and USB-A ports on the Raspberry Pi.

Q: I'm not sure which port on my Pi is the right one for the display cable. What should I look for?
A: Look for a port labelled DISPLAY, CAM/DISP, or DISP on your Raspberry Pi.

Q: My Pi has two DSI connectors. Which one should I use?
A: Use the port labelled 1 if your Raspberry Pi has multiple DSI connectors.

Q: What happens if I plug the power cable in wrong?
A: Connecting the power cable incorrectly might cause damage to the display.

Q: Which GPIO pins does the power cable connect to?
A: The red cable connects to pin 2 and the black ground cable connects to pin 6.

Q: Can I physically attach my Pi to the back of the display?
A: Yes, optionally use the included M2.5 screws to mount your Raspberry Pi to the back.

# Use an on-screen keyboard

Q: Do I need to install a keyboard app or does one come with Raspberry Pi OS already?
A: Raspberry Pi OS Bookworm and later already includes the Squeekboard on-screen keyboard.

Q: The on-screen keyboard isn't showing up automatically. How do I bring it up?
A: Tap the keyboard icon on the right side of the taskbar to manually show it.

Q: How do I turn off the on-screen keyboard permanently?
A: Go to Preferences, Control Centre, Display, or use raspi-config in the terminal.

# Change screen orientation

Q: The display is in portrait mode but I want it sideways. How do I rotate it?
A: In the desktop, go to Preferences, Screen Configuration, right-click your display, and choose Orientation.

Q: What are my rotation options for the display?
A: You can rotate 0, 90, 180, or 270 degrees clockwise from the default portrait orientation.

Q: I'm running without a desktop. Can I still rotate the screen?
A: Yes, add a video rotation parameter to the end of the cmdline.txt file in boot firmware.

Q: I rotated the screen in cmdline.txt but my video player isn't rotating. What's going on?
A: Apps writing directly to DRM won't use that rotation and need their own rotation options.

Q: Can I rotate the DSI display to a different angle than my HDMI display?
A: No, when using DSI and HDMI simultaneously they share the same rotation value in cmdline.txt.

# Customise touchscreen settings

Q: The touch input seems off — like it's detecting taps in the wrong direction. How do I fix that?
A: Use the invx or invy Device Tree overlay parameters to flip the touch axes.

Q: How do I disable the touchscreen but keep the display working?
A: Add disable_touch to the dtoverlay line in the boot configuration file.

Q: Where do I actually put these touchscreen settings?
A: Add the Device Tree parameters to the dtoverlay line in /boot/firmware/config.txt.

Q: What overlay name do I use for the seven-inch display?
A: For the seven-inch display, the overlay is called vc4-kms-dsi-ili9881-7inch.

Q: I want to swap the X and Y axes on the touch. Is there a setting for that?
A: Yes, use the swapxy parameter in the dtoverlay line to swap touch X and Y axes.

# Connect to a Compute Module

Q: I've got a Compute Module and the display isn't working. Do I need to configure something manually?
A: Yes, Compute Modules don't auto-detect the display and require manual Device Tree configuration.

Q: Why does the display work automatically on a normal Pi but not on my Compute Module?
A: Standard Raspberry Pi boards have fixed DSI connections and auto-detect displays; Compute Modules expose all interfaces for flexibility but can't auto-detect.

Q: What's the easiest way to set up the Touch Display 2 on a Compute Module?
A: The simplest option is adding an overlay entry in the config.txt file.

A: You get the display, eight M2.5 screws, two FFCs, and a GPIO power cable.

A: It's designed for tablets, entertainment systems, and information dashboards.
