# Raspberry Pi Touch Display

Q: What's the resolution on the Raspberry Pi touch display?
A: The display has an 800 by 480 pixel RGB LCD screen.

Q: Does the touch display work with a Pi Zero?
A: No, the Zero series lacks a DSI connector and isn't compatible.

Q: How many touch points does the Raspberry Pi touch display support?
A: It supports 10-point multi-touch input on the touchscreen.

Q: How long does the backlight last before it wears out?
A: The backlight has a rated lifetime of 20,000 hours.

Q: What are the operating temperatures for this display?
A: It operates in temperatures from negative 20 to positive 70 degrees Celsius.

Q: How much power does the touch display use?
A: It typically draws 200 milliamps at 5 volts at maximum brightness.

# Mount the Touch Display

Q: How do I attach my Pi to the back of the touch display?
A: Mount the Pi using the display's stand-offs and connect the appropriate cables.

Q: I connected the display but there's nothing showing up. What should I check first?
A: Always double-check that the Flat Flexible Cable is fully and correctly inserted.

Q: Which way do the contacts face when I plug in the ribbon cable?
A: Contacts face away from the display on the display end, inward on the Pi end.

Q: I'm putting the display in a separate case. Can I still do that?
A: Yes, you can mount the display in a separate chassis with longer cables.

# Power the Touch Display

Q: What's the best way to power the touch display?
A: Raspberry Pi recommends powering the display from the Pi's GPIO pins.

Q: Which GPIO pins do I use to power the touch display from my Pi?
A: Connect pin four for 5V and pin six for ground on the Pi's GPIO header.

Q: Can I use a different ground pin if pin six is taken?
A: Yes, any available ground pin works for the black wire connection.

Q: I don't want to use GPIO for power. Can I just use a USB power supply instead?
A: Yes, you can use a separate micro USB power supply for the display instead.

Q: If I'm powering the display with micro USB, do I still connect the GPIO pins?
A: No, with micro USB power, only the Flat Flexible Cable should connect the two boards.

Q: What power supply do you recommend for powering the display over micro USB?
A: Raspberry Pi recommends the official 12.5 watt micro USB power supply.

# Use an on-screen keyboard

Q: I've got a touchscreen but there's no keyboard showing up. How do I get one?
A: Raspberry Pi OS Bookworm includes Squeekboard, which appears automatically when needed.

Q: The keyboard isn't popping up automatically in my app. How do I show it manually?
A: Tap the keyboard icon at the right end of the taskbar to show or hide it.

Q: Can I just leave the on-screen keyboard showing all the time?
A: Yes, permanently show or hide it in the Display tab of Control Centre.

Q: I'm running an older version of Raspberry Pi OS. What keyboard should I use?
A: Use matchbox-keyboard on releases prior to Bookworm, or wvkbd for wayfire compositor.

# Change screen orientation

Q: My display is mounted upside down. How do I rotate it in the software?
A: Open Screen Configuration from Preferences, right-click the display, and choose Orientation.

Q: I'm running without a desktop. How do I rotate the screen?
A: Edit cmdline.txt and add the video equals DSI-1 line with your desired rotation value.

Q: What rotation values can I actually use in cmdline.txt?
A: You can use 0, 90, 180, or 270 degrees of rotation.

Q: I've got both HDMI and the DSI display running. Can I rotate them separately?
A: No, both displays share the same rotation value when used simultaneously via cmdline.txt.

# Rotate touch input

Q: My screen is rotated but touch input is still wrong. How do I fix that?
A: Add a dtoverlay instruction with invx and invy in your config.txt file.

Q: Do I need to change anything else in config.txt when I add the touch rotation overlay?
A: Yes, remove the display_auto_detect equals 1 line from config.txt if it exists.

Q: Is rotating the touch input the same as rotating the display?
A: No, touch input rotation is independent from the display's orientation setting.

# Touch Display device tree option reference

Q: Can I change the resolution of the touch display in software?
A: Yes, use sizex and sizey parameters in the dtoverlay line in config.txt.

Q: How do I turn off touch completely if I don't need it?
A: Add disable_touch to your dtoverlay line in config.txt to disable touch entirely.

Q: I want to flip both X and Y and also set a custom width. How do I write that line?
A: Add sizex equals your value then invx and invy separated by commas in dtoverlay.

# Installation on Compute Module based devices

Q: Why doesn't the touch display auto-detect on my Compute Module like it does on a regular Pi?
A: Compute Modules disable autodetection since integrators may use any GPIO for custom purposes.

Q: How do I get the touch display working on a Compute Module then?
A: Add a dtoverlay entry in config.txt, use a custom base DT file, or use a HAT EEPROM.

# Legacy Support

Q: I've got an original Model B Pi. Can I use the touch display with it?
A: Yes, but you need extra jumper cables for SCL and SDA connections to the GPIO header.

Q: Why does the original Pi need extra cables that newer Pis don't?
A: The original Pi's DSI connector lacks I2C connections needed for touchscreen and DSI controllers.

Q: My original Pi isn't detecting the display. Is there a setting I need to turn on?
A: Add ignore_lcd equals 0 to your config.txt to enable display detection.

Q: Can I power the display setup through the Pi's micro USB on the original Model B?
A: No, power through the display's PWR IN connector; the display draws too much current otherwise.
