# Raspberry Pi Monitor

Q: What size is the Raspberry Pi Monitor and how does it connect?
A: It's a 15.6 inch 1080p display that connects via HDMI cable.

Q: Does this monitor work with all Raspberry Pi models?
A: Yes, it's compatible with all Raspberry Pi models that support HDMI output.

Q: What do I need to power the monitor? Can I just use any USB charger?
A: It needs a USB-C power source, ideally USB-PD capable of at least 1.5A.

# Controls

Q: Where are the buttons on this monitor? I can't find them.
A: The buttons are on the back, controlling standby, brightness, and volume.

Q: How do I put the monitor into standby? Is there a button for that?
A: Press the power symbol button on the back to enter or exit standby.

Q: I want to turn down the brightness — where's that control?
A: Use the sun symbol buttons on the back to increase or decrease brightness.

# On screen display messages

Q: My monitor just showed some weird message on screen. What does it mean when it says no HDMI signal?
A: It means the monitor isn't detecting any HDMI signal from a connected device.

Q: I'm getting a message about unsupported resolution. What's going on?
A: Your connected device is outputting a display resolution the monitor doesn't support.

Q: The screen showed something about power saving mode. Why is that happening?
A: The monitor isn't connected to a power supply capable of delivering 1.5A or greater.

Q: I tried pressing the standby button to wake it up but nothing happened, just a red light?
A: The red LED lights briefly when no HDMI signal is detected; display stays in standby.

Q: Does the volume and brightness go from zero to a hundred on that little on-screen display?
A: Yes, both brightness and volume use a scale from 0 to 100.

# Position the Monitor

Q: Can I mount this monitor on a VESA arm?
A: Yes, use the four VESA mount holes on the back of the red rear housing.

Q: I want to hang the monitor on a wall without a VESA mount. Is there another way?
A: Flip the stand up and hang it using two drill holes spaced 55mm apart.

Q: Do I need spacers if I'm mounting it on an arm?
A: Yes, use spacers to ensure adequate space for display and power cable egress.

# Power the Monitor

Q: Can I just plug my Raspberry Pi into the monitor's USB-C port to power it?
A: Powering from a Raspberry Pi puts the monitor in Power Saving mode due to insufficient current.

Q: What's the difference between Full Power mode and Power Saving mode?
A: Full Power allows 0-100% brightness and volume; Power Saving limits them to lower ranges.

Q: My monitor keeps flashing on and off. What's causing that?
A: Your USB power supply can't provide sufficient current to power the monitor reliably.

Q: I'm in Power Saving mode but I want full brightness. How do I switch?
A: Press and hold the increase brightness button for 3 seconds to switch modes.

Q: Will the official Raspberry Pi 4 power supply work for full brightness on this monitor?
A: Yes, the official Raspberry Pi 4 power supply supports USB-PD and works for full power.

Q: I'm in Power Saving mode — how loud can I get the speakers and how bright can the screen go?
A: Power Saving mode limits brightness to 0-50% and speaker volume to 0-60%.

Q: My monitor's flashing and I can't change settings because I can't see anything. What do I do?
A: Connect the monitor to another power supply to access brightness and volume settings.

# Specification

Q: Does this monitor have built-in speakers?
A: Yes, it has two 1.2 watt stereo speakers.

Q: What resolutions does this monitor support?
A: It supports 1920×1080, 1280×720, 720×576, 720×480, and 640×480 at 50 or 60Hz.

Q: Is there a headphone jack on this monitor?
A: Yes, there's a 3.5mm audio jack.

Q: How heavy is this monitor? I need to know for mounting.
A: The Raspberry Pi Monitor weighs 850 grams.

Q: What's the contrast ratio on this monitor?
A: The contrast ratio is 800:1.

# Whole Document

Q: I just got a Raspberry Pi Monitor — what do I actually need to set it up?
A: You need an HDMI cable and a USB-C USB-PD power source providing at least 1.5A.

Q: Can I use a USB-A cable to power this monitor at full brightness?
A: No, USB-A sources put the monitor in Power Saving mode with reduced brightness and volume.

Q: I've got a Raspberry Pi 5 — will it power this monitor okay?
A: Powering from a Raspberry Pi puts the monitor in Power Saving mode, not full power.
